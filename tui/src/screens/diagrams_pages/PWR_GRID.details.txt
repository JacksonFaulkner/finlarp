== TRANSMISSION
# What it does
Transmission lines carry power long distances at 115 to 345 kV (and higher), strung from steel towers between power plants and substations.
# Why it matters
High voltage keeps current and losses low; a campus is limited by the capacity of the lines that reach it.
# Source
Textbook.

== THREE-PHASE
# What it does
Three-phase power uses three conductors whose voltages peak one third of a cycle apart, so the total power delivered is steady.
# Why it matters
It moves more power per conductor than single-phase and runs motors smoothly; every stage down to the rack's power shelves is three-phase AC.
# Source
Textbook.

== UTILITY
# What it does
The utility owns the transmission and distribution wires to a site and sells it power, under rates set by regulators.
# Why it matters
It decides how fast a campus can connect and how much power it gets, so data center builders now negotiate directly for capacity years ahead.
# Source
Textbook.

== INTERCONNECTION QUEUE
# What it does
The interconnection queue is the list of generation and storage projects waiting for grid operators to study and approve their connection.
# Why it matters
Berkeley Lab counted about 1,400 GW of generation and 890 GW of storage queued at the end of 2024; projects built in 2024 took 55 months on average.
# Source
LBNL, Queued Up 2025 Edition.

== DATA CENTER LOAD
# What it does
US data centers used about 176 TWh in 2023, 4.4% of US electricity, up from 58 TWh in 2014.
# Why it matters
Berkeley Lab projects 325-580 TWh by 2028, 6.7% to 12% of US electricity, driven mostly by AI servers.
# Source
LBNL / DOE, December 2024.

== PUE
# What it does
Power usage effectiveness is total site power divided by the power reaching IT equipment; 1.0 would mean no cooling or conversion overhead.
# Why it matters
At 1.2, a 100 MW IT load needs 120 MW from the grid; cooling and losses take the rest, so PUE sets how many chips a grid connection can feed.
# Source
Textbook (The Green Grid definition).

== BEHIND THE METER
# What it does
Behind-the-meter power is generated on the site itself (gas turbines, engines, fuel cells) or delivered by a dedicated line from a nearby plant.
# Why it matters
It lets a campus open without waiting years for grid upgrades, at the cost of running its own plant and its own fuel supply.
# Source
Textbook.

== GAS TURBINE
# What it does
A gas turbine burns natural gas to spin a generator; aeroderivative and heavy-duty units range from tens to hundreds of megawatts.
# Why it matters
They are the usual way to power a large campus on site; turbine order books have grown long as AI demand rises.
# Source
Textbook.
