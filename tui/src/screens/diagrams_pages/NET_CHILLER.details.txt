== CHILLER
# What it does
A chiller is a refrigeration machine that cools a loop of water for the building, using a refrigerant cycle and rejecting the heat outdoors.
# Why it matters
Most air-cooled and many liquid-cooled data halls depend on chilled water; chillers are one of the largest power users after the IT itself.
# Source
ASHRAE Handbook.

== CHILLED WATER
# What it does
Chilled water is the building loop that carries heat from air handlers or CDUs back to the chiller.
# Why it matters
Its temperature sets the chiller's efficiency: warmer water (15-20 C, or 30 C+ for liquid cooling) means less compressor work.
# Source
ASHRAE TC 9.9.

== EVAPORATOR
# What it does
The evaporator is a shell-and-tube heat exchanger where low-pressure refrigerant boils around tubes carrying the water.
# Why it matters
This is where the water gives up its heat.
# Source
Textbook.

== COMPRESSOR
# What it does
The compressor squeezes refrigerant vapour to high pressure and temperature; screw and centrifugal types are common in chillers.
# Why it matters
It uses most of the chiller's power; magnetic-bearing centrifugals are the most efficient.
# Source
Textbook; Danfoss Turbocor.

== CONDENSER
# What it does
The condenser sheds the refrigerant's heat to outdoor air (finned coils and fans) or to tower water (a shell-and-tube barrel).
# Why it matters
It rejects the IT heat plus the compressor's power.
# Source
Textbook.

== EXPANSION VALVE
# What it does
The expansion valve drops the liquid refrigerant's pressure so part of it flashes to vapour and the mix turns cold.
# Why it matters
It meters refrigerant into the evaporator and closes the loop.
# Source
Textbook.

== REFRIGERANT
# What it does
The working fluid that boils and condenses inside the chiller: R-134a, R-410A, and low-GWP R-1234ze, R-513A, R-1233zd.
# Why it matters
HFC phase-down rules force a move to low-GWP refrigerants in new chillers.
# Source
EPA Technology Transitions rule.

== FREE COOLING
# What it does
Free cooling uses cold outdoor air (an extra coil, or a separate economizer) to chill the water while the compressor rests.
# Why it matters
With warm-water liquid cooling it can cover most hours of the year, cutting cooling energy sharply.
# Source
ASHRAE; Vertiv, Modine product literature.

== DRY COOLER
# What it does
A dry cooler is a finned coil with fans and no compressor; it can only cool water to a few degrees above the air temperature.
# Why it matters
It uses no water and suits 30-45 C liquid-cooling loops; adiabatic pads help on hot days.
# Source
Modine 10-K FY2026.
