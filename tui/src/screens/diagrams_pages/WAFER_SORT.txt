# 3. Procedure and physics

  1  Load wafer from FOUP, find notch, optical pre-align, vacuum onto chuck
  2  Contact: chuck rises until needle tips touch; overdrive adds Z travel so tips bend and scrub the
      pad oxide
     too little overdrive: bad contact; too much: pad damage or tip sliding off the pad
  3  Tester applies power and patterns: DC (shorts/opens, leakage), functional, speed bins, analog trims
  4  Result per die is written to the wafer map as a bin number; the chuck steps to the next site (or
      next group)
  5  Repair step for memory: failing rows/columns replaced from spares, then retest (textbook knowledge)
  6  Temperature: hot or cold chuck used to screen temperature-sensitive fails (textbook knowledge)

# 5. By the numbers

  Probes per die, adv. packaging devices    1K to 5K probes per die (FormFactor SWTW deck)
  Probes, advanced packaging SoC cards      15K to 60K in total on the card (FormFactor deck)
  HBM parallelism                           128 to 256 sites at once (FormFactor deck)
  HBM micro-bump probing pitch              below 60 um; fine pitch 45 um with probe force under 1.5 gf
  1995 high-end card                        500 probes, 60 um tips, 100 um pads on 125 um pitch
      (Keithley note)
  Overdrive example in a patent             about 3 mils (~76 um)
  Per-die test time                         no sourced number found; it varies from ms to seconds by
      device

# 8. Who makes it, chokepoints

  Testers          Advantest (6857.T, ADR ATEYY), Teradyne (TER); smaller: Cohu (COHU), Keysight (KEYS)
  Probe cards      FormFactor (FORM), Technoprobe (TPRO.MI), also MPI, CHPT, Micronics Japan (6871.T)
  Probers          Tokyo Electron (8035.T), Accretech / Tokyo Seimitsu (7729.T) (textbook knowledge, no
      sourced share)
  Drivers          AI accelerators, networking and HBM/DRAM testing push both tester and probe card
      demand
  Risk             tester duopoly makes capacity of a few lines a gate for AI chip ramps; HBM
      known-good-die need
                   makes fine-pitch probe cards a single-digit-vendor bottleneck
