== CDU
# What it does
A coolant distribution unit moves heat from the servers' sealed liquid loop into facility water, and holds that loop's temperature, pressure and flow.
# Why it matters
Every direct-to-chip liquid-cooled rack needs one; as rack power climbs, CDU capacity is a gating item.
# Source
OCP ACS guidance; vendor product pages.

== SECONDARY LOOP
# What it does
The sealed loop from the CDU through the rack's manifolds and cold plates; OCP calls it the technology cooling system (TCS).
# Why it matters
Its small volume of clean, treated coolant keeps building water and debris out of the servers.
# Source
OCP ACS.

== FACILITY LOOP
# What it does
The building's water loop that takes heat from the CDU to chillers, dry coolers or cooling towers.
# Why it matters
Warmer allowed facility water means less chiller energy, which the PHE's small approach makes possible.
# Source
Textbook.

== PLATE HEAT EXCHANGER
# What it does
A stack of thin pressed plates with the two fluids in alternate gaps, usually counterflow.
# Why it matters
It sets how much heat the CDU can move and how close supply gets to facility water.
# Source
Textbook; Alfa Laval.

== APPROACH
# What it does
The temperature gap between the cool secondary supply leaving the PHE and the facility water entering it, typically about 2-4 C.
# Why it matters
A smaller approach lets warmer facility water do the job.
# Source
Textbook.

== PUMP
# What it does
Variable-speed centrifugal pumps drive the secondary loop, usually N+1 so one can fail.
# Why it matters
Losing flow overheats GPUs within seconds, so pumps are redundant and hot-swappable.
# Source
Vendor product pages.

== FILTER
# What it does
A strainer on the secondary loop, often around 25-50 micron.
# Why it matters
Cold plate micro-channels clog easily; filtration protects them.
# Source
OCP ACS.

== RESERVOIR
# What it does
An expansion tank holding spare coolant and taking up its swell as it warms.
# Why it matters
It keeps the pumps fed and the loop pressure steady.
# Source
Textbook.

== CONTROLLER
# What it does
A PLC reading temperature, pressure, flow and leak sensors, driving pump speed and the facility valve.
# Why it matters
It holds supply conditions and fails over pumps without a person in the loop.
# Source
Vendor product pages.

== COLD PLATE
# What it does
A copper block with fine internal channels pressed onto a GPU or CPU, carrying its heat into the coolant.
# Why it matters
It is where the heat enters the liquid loop.
# Source
Textbook.

== QUICK DISCONNECT
# What it does
A dripless coupling joining each tray's hoses to the rack manifold.
# Why it matters
It lets trays be pulled without draining the loop, and it is a common leak point.
# Source
OCP ACS.

== DEW POINT
# What it does
The temperature below which moisture in the room air condenses on a cold surface.
# Why it matters
The CDU keeps its supply above it so pipes and cold plates stay dry.
# Source
Textbook.

== IN-RACK CDU
# What it does
A few-U CDU in the bottom of one rack with its own small exchanger and pumps, roughly 100-250 kW.
# Why it matters
No floor space and simple deployment, but sized to one rack. Supermicro sells a 250 kW unit.
# Source
Supermicro.

== IN-ROW CDU
# What it does
A full cabinet in the row feeding many racks through headers, from hundreds of kW to 2 MW or more.
# Why it matters
Fewer, bigger units with shared redundancy; Vertiv, Schneider/Motivair and nVent lead here.
# Source
Vertiv; vendor pages.
