# 1. What happens: build the gate dielectric and metal stack (gate-last HKMG)

The gate is a stack of films, each grown by atomic layer deposition (ALD), one layer per cycle. IL GROWTH: a chemical SiOx interlayer about 0.5 nm thick. HIGH-K ALD: an HfO2 film about 1.5-2 nm thick. TiN CAP: protection during anneal. DIPOLE: a La or Al cap driven in to shift the threshold. WORK-FUNCTION: TiN or TiAlC layers that set Vt. METAL FILL: tungsten (or cobalt), then polish flat.

# 2. The replacement-metal-gate flow

DUMMY POLY: a polysilicon placeholder gate on SiO2. SPACER + S/D: implant and epitaxy, annealed at about 1000 C. ILD + CMP: oxide fill, polished to expose the poly top. POLY PULL: etch out the dummy, leaving a trench. RMG STACK: grow the IL, HfO2 and work-function metals in the trench, fill and polish. All the hot source/drain work is done before high-k and metal exist, so they never see it.

# 3. One replacement gate in section

From the bottom of the trench up: IL SiOx about 0.5 nm on the silicon; HfO2 plus a dipole cap (k about 20-25, EOT about 1 nm class); the work-function metal, PMOS WFM TiN near the valence band and NMOS WFM TiAlC(N), Al-rich, near the conduction band; a TiN glue and barrier that blocks tungsten diffusion; and W FILL (low resistance), which sets the gate resistance. The layers line the trench floor and walls as U-shapes with the fill in the middle; spacers and ILD0 oxide on both sides.

# 4. Gate-first versus gate-last

GATE-FIRST: the stack goes in first, then a poly cap and the source/drain anneal, so the high-k sees about 1000 C (IBM Common Platform, 32/28 nm). Denser and simpler, but the threshold shifts with the thermal budget. GATE-LAST: dummy poly first, the stack added after the source/drain, so it never sees hot steps (Intel 45 nm, TSMC 28 nm). Gate-last won as nodes shrank.

# 5. Why the oxide had to go: EOT and leakage

SiO2 gate oxide thinner than about 1.2 nm at the 90 nm node leaked unacceptably by direct tunnelling. EOT = t_highk x (3.9 / k_highk) + t_IL x (3.9 / k_IL), where 3.9 is SiO2's k: a physically thicker HfO2 gives the same capacitance with far less tunnelling. Intel's 45 nm HKMG used HfO2 at about 1.0 nm EOT for over 10x lower gate leakage and about 20% more drive current. The IL has low k, so every 0.1 nm of IL eats EOT budget; scaling EOT now means thinning the IL.

# 6. Threshold tuning in a nanosheet gate

In GAA the gate wraps all four sides of each sheet, and the gaps between sheets are only a few nm, so IL, HfO2 and work-function metal must all fit. Multiple thresholds come from mixing dipole caps and work-function metal thickness and composition: La-based caps lower Vt on nFETs, Al-based raise it on pFETs (general literature, not verified by a source here; recipes vary by foundry).

# 8. Who makes it

ASM International (ASM.AS) leads ALD, with HKMG reactors and claimed leading single-wafer ALD share. Applied Materials (AMAT): Centura dielectric stack, Endura PVD/ALD TiN and metal-gate integration. Lam Research (LRCX): ALD tungsten (ALTUS), ALTUS Halo molybdenum aimed at GAA. Tokyo Electron (8035.T): furnace and ALD tools. Foundries: TSMC (28 nm gate-last), Intel (45 nm, 2007); Synopsys and Cadence model the threshold flavours.

# 9. Chokepoints and risks

- Threshold variability in tiny GAA gaps.
- ALD uniformity and interlayer thickness control.
- Fill voids as gates shrink.
