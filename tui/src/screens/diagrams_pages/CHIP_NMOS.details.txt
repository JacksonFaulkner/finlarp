== OFF STATE
# Gate at zero
With VG = 0 and the source at 0 V, the p-type body under the gate has plenty of holes and almost no electrons. The two n+ regions look like back-to-back diodes to each other, so no continuous electron path exists (textbook). What flows is only leakage: subthreshold current over the barrier, reverse junction current, and gate tunnelling.

# Why leakage matters
A chip has 1e10 to 2e11 switches that are mostly off at any instant. A tiny Ioff per device times that count becomes watts of static power, which is why Vt, halo doping and gate dielectrics are tuned together. Failure modes: Vt too low (leaky), high-temperature runaway, and DIBL at short channels.

# Companies
TSMC (TSM) 2025 revenue NT$3,809B, about US$122.4B; Intel (INTC) and Samsung ship competing leakage-tuned Vt menus. Intel 18A offers 4 NMOS Vt flavours over a 180 mV range.

== DEPLETION
# Mechanism
A small positive gate voltage repels holes from the surface of the p-type body, leaving a region of fixed negative boron ions with no free carriers (textbook). The depletion width grows with gate voltage until electrons begin to collect, at which point it stops growing.

# Depletion capacitance
That depleted layer acts as a capacitor Cdep in series with the body. The ratio Cdep/Cox enters the subthreshold swing S = (kT/q) ln10 (1 + Cdep/Cox), so a thin depletion layer or a fully depleted channel (FinFET, GAA) gets closer to the 60 mV/decade floor.

# Why fully depleted won
Planar bulk NMOS needs heavy channel doping to control short-channel effects, which scatters carriers and adds random dopant variation. Thin undoped fins and nanosheets avoid that. Beneficiary is the foundry stack: TSM, INTC, Samsung.

== INVERSION
# What inverts
Above the threshold voltage the surface potential reaches about twice the Fermi potential and a thin sheet of electrons forms under the oxide (textbook). That sheet is the n-type channel linking source and drain. It is only 1-3 nm thick in planar devices and sits in the silicon next to the gate dielectric.

# Why NMOS is faster
Electrons have higher mobility than holes in silicon (textbook: roughly 2-3x), so NMOS delivers more drive current than PMOS of the same width. PMOS must be made wider or strained harder to match, which is why CMOS cells have unequal widths.

# Failure modes
Surface roughness scattering, remote Coulomb scattering from the high-k layer, and self-heating all cut mobility and so drive current. Gate-all-around channels add quantum confinement effects.

== LINEAR
# Resistor regime
When the drain voltage is small compared with the gate overdrive (VG - Vt), the channel behaves like a gate-controlled resistor and current grows linearly with VD (textbook). Id is about mu Cox (W/L)(VG - Vt) VD.

# Uses
Pass gates, SRAM access transistors and analogue switches operate here. The on-resistance sets the RC delay of local wiring, which, together with wire resistance, limits speed. External resistance (contacts, S/D epi) is now a large fraction of the total; Intel quotes -20% (NMOS) external resistance on 18A-P vs 18A.

# Sizing
Wider W or shorter L lowers resistance; more fins or sheets add width in discrete steps. Standard-cell libraries choose fin/sheet counts. EDA vendors SNPS and CDNS model this; FY2025 revenue $7.054B and $5.297B.

== SATURATION
# Pinch-off
When VD exceeds VG - Vt the channel pinches off near the drain and extra voltage drops across a short depleted region; current becomes nearly independent of VD (textbook). Digital logic drives its outputs in this regime.

# Velocity saturation
In nanometre devices carriers hit a saturation velocity (about 1e7 cm/s in silicon, textbook) before pinch-off, so Id follows roughly (VG - Vt)^a with a near 1-1.3 instead of the long-channel square law. Drive current is therefore set by injection velocity at the source, not by mobility alone.

# What boosts it
Strain (Si:P), lower external resistance and higher gate-stack capacitance. Intel reports about +5% NMOS and +16% PMOS drive on 18A-P vs 18A.

== TURN-OFF
# Switching energy
To turn off, the gate is discharged and electrons drain back out of the channel. Dynamic power is about C V^2 f; each switching event charges the gate and wire capacitance (textbook). Lower VDD is the main power lever, which is why Vt and swing matter.

# Limits
Because swing is at least 60 mV/dec, dropping VDD below roughly 0.5 V forces Ioff up sharply. Real devices show body factor m of 1.2-1.5, i.e. 70-90 mV/dec.

# Hold-time failures
Slow turn-off from RC and bias-temperature drift (NBTI mostly hits PMOS, PBTI hits high-k NMOS, textbook) shift Vt over years and eat timing margin. TSM, INTC and Samsung publish reliability targets in 10-year lifetimes.

== N+ SOURCE
# Role
The source is the electron reservoir. Heavily doped (about 1e20 to 1e21 per cm3 for n+, textbook) so it is metal-like, with low resistance and a shallow extension under the gate edge. Electrons are injected over a barrier whose height the gate sets.

# Construction
An implanted extension (low energy, shallow) is followed by deep source/drain. In FinFET and GAA nodes the S/D is epitaxial Si:P grown in etched cavities beside the fins or sheets; the epi doubles as a strain source. A silicide or metal contact lands on top.

# Parasitics
Source resistance is series resistance that degrades drive current. Failure modes: dopant deactivation after anneals, contact-resistivity limits (1.5e-9 Ohm.cm2 reported on 2e21 /cm3 Si:P) and epi merging between neighbouring fins. Equipment: Applied Materials (AMAT) epi, Axcelis (ACLS) implant.

== GATE STACK
# Stack
A metal gate on a high-k dielectric on a thin interfacial oxide. Equivalent oxide thickness is about 1 nm class (textbook). The work function metals set Vt: Intel 18A uses dipole-based tuning to offer eight Vt options over a 180 mV range (4 NMOS, 4 PMOS).

# Why not poly-Si/SiO2
Gate leakage by direct tunnelling blew up when SiO2 dropped under about 1.5 nm (textbook); high-k plus metal gate (introduced at 45 nm, textbook) restored control. Poly depletion also disappeared with metal.

# Variability
Work-function grain variation and thickness variation produce Vt mismatch; SRAM cells fail first. Companies: TSM, INTC, Samsung for process; AMAT and Lam (LRCX) for ALD and etch; Synopsys and Cadence for modelling.

== N+ DRAIN
# Role
The drain collects the electrons and connects to the output node or to the next gate. It is built like the source but sees the high field: peak field near the drain edge heats carriers (hot-carrier injection).

# Failure modes
Hot carriers damage the gate dielectric and shift Vt over time. Drain-induced barrier lowering lets VD reduce the barrier at the source. Gate-induced drain leakage (GIDL) flows band-to-band at the overlap when the gate is off. Lightly doped extensions and halo pockets trade these against series resistance.

# Symmetry
Planar NMOS is symmetric; source and drain swap by bias. In logic gates the lower-potential terminal is the source.

== P-WELL
# What it is
A p-type region (boron doped) built in the n-type or p-type wafer so NMOS devices sit in a doped well with the right Vt and isolation. Doping around 1e15 to 1e18 per cm3 (textbook). In CMOS an n-well holds PMOS next to the p-well holding NMOS.

# Latch-up and isolation
The parasitic npn/pnp thyristor between wells can latch if tapped wrongly, shorting VDD to ground; guard rings and well taps prevent that. STI (shallow trench isolation) separates neighbours; triple-well isolates sensitive blocks.

# Why FinFET/GAA change it
Fins and sheets are mostly undoped, so the well is replaced by a punch-through stopper below the fin or by a bottom dielectric isolation. Wafer supplier: Shin-Etsu and SUMCO (textbook, tickers 4063.T and 3436.T not sourced).

== THRESHOLD
# Definition
The gate voltage at which a strong inversion layer forms. Vt = VFB + 2*phiF + sqrt(2 q eps Na (2 phiF))/Cox (textbook). Chip designers use a menu of Vt flavours: ultra-low (fast, leaky), standard and high (slow, low leakage).

# Knobs
Channel doping, gate work function, dielectric thickness and body bias. Intel 18A has four NMOS Vt flavours, 180 mV total span; 18A-P adds a fifth logic Vt pair between ULVT and LVT.

# Failure modes
Vt scatter from dopant fluctuation, work-function grains and line-edge roughness sets minimum SRAM voltage. Vt roll-off at short L and bias-temperature shift hurt margins.

== SUBTHRESHOLD
# Swing
Below Vt the current is diffusion over a barrier and varies exponentially with gate voltage: S = (kT/q) ln10 (1 + Cdep/Cox), about 60 mV/decade at 300 K for an ideal device (kT/q about 26 mV). Practical bulk devices have m = 1.2-1.5, so 70-90 mV/dec.

# Why it limits scaling
Because S has a thermal floor, lowering Vt 100 mV costs about 10-17x more Ioff, so supply voltage cannot fall freely. This is the Boltzmann tyranny that motivates negative-capacitance and tunnel-FET research (textbook).

# Who fights it
Wrap-around gates raise gate control so Cdep/Cox falls: FinFET (TSM, INTC, Samsung) and nanosheets get close to 60-70 mV/dec in good cases (textbook).

== Si:P
# Why
Silicon phosphorus epitaxy grown in S/D cavities gives very high active n-type doping and, because P is smaller than Si, tensile strain in the channel for NMOS. A patent example reached P at 1.2e21 /cm3 with resistivity 0.3 mOhm.cm and strain like about 0.55% substitutional carbon.

# Limits
Above about 1e21 /cm3 active carrier concentration saturates, and activation anneals can relax strain. Contact resistivity as low as 1.5e-9 Ohm.cm2 was reported on 2e21 /cm3 Si:P with a dynamic surface anneal. Low-temperature (450 C) tensile Si:P grown by Applied Materials reached 0.21 mOhm.cm at 5.8% P.

# Tool vendors
Epitaxy: Applied Materials (AMAT), ASM International, Kokusai. Companies using it: TSM, INTC, Samsung. GAA nodes shift the strain mechanism since the channel is a suspended sheet.

== ARSENIC
# Why arsenic
Arsenic is a heavy atom with low diffusivity, so implanted extensions stay shallow and abrupt, which helps short-channel behaviour. Its drawback is poor activation and high sheet resistance. Phosphorus activates better but diffuses faster and goes deeper; a P+As co-implant gives gentler junction profiles with As keeping contact resistance low.

# Implant steps
A halo (p-type pocket) is implanted at tilt below each n+ extension to block punch-through. Energies for P extensions of 400-1500 eV give junctions about 8-18 nm deep (patent examples). Anneals (spike, laser) set activation.

# Suppliers
Axcelis (ACLS) is the pure-play implanter; Applied Materials (AMAT) also sells implant. Failure modes: transient-enhanced diffusion, channeling, damage not fully annealed, dopant segregation.

== DIBL
# What
Drain-induced barrier lowering: at short channel length the drain field penetrates to the source and lowers the barrier, so Vt drops as VD rises. It raises Ioff and wrecks analogue gain.

# Threshold roll-off
Vt also falls with shorter channel length as the junction depth to channel length ratio grows. Both worsen as L shrinks and are reduced by thin channels, halo doping, and gates that wrap the channel.

# Why architecture changed
Planar bulk could not keep DIBL acceptable below roughly 20 nm gate lengths (textbook), driving FinFET then GAA. TSM, INTC, Samsung all moved to GAA at 2nm class.

== BODY BIAS
# Mechanism
Applying a voltage between source and body shifts Vt: reverse body bias (negative on NMOS body) raises Vt and cuts leakage; forward bias lowers Vt and speeds up (textbook). Vt shift follows a square-root law in the source-body voltage.

# Use
Used for adaptive voltage and leakage control, e.g. standby leakage cut or speed boost, mostly in FD-SOI and in some bulk designs. In FinFET and GAA the body is thin, so body effect is weak and back bias is rarely used.

# Failure modes
Forward bias beyond about 0.5 V turns on the body diode; latch-up risk increases. Companies: GlobalFoundries (GFS) and Samsung offer FD-SOI with body bias (textbook, not sourced).

== TRANSISTOR COUNT
# Figures
NVIDIA B200: 208 billion transistors on two dies, TSMC 4NP, versus 80 billion for H100/H200. AMD Instinct MI300X: 153 billion across 12 chiplets (5nm and 6nm). Apple M4: 28 billion on TSMC N3E; M3 25B, M2 20B, M1 16B.

# Why it grows
Count scales with die area, density gains per node and now packaging that stitches chiplets. Even so, most of a GPU's switches are in caches and SMs, NMOS-PMOS pairs. A reticle-limited die is about 800 mm2.

# Revenue context
NVIDIA FY2026 revenue $215.9B, data center $193.7B. TSMC 2025 revenue NT$3,809B (about US$122.4B), 3nm 24% of wafer revenue, advanced nodes 74%. AMD (AMD) builds MI300X; Apple's chips are TSM-made.

# Caveat
Press transistor counts are vendor numbers, are counted differently (some include SRAM, some include HBM dies), and A18 Pro was reported as 20B by a single site only.
