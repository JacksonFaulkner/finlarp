# 1. What happens: warm water falls through air, heat leaves as vapour

A water-cooled chiller's CONDENSER sends warm water, around 35 C, to the COOLING TOWER. The water is sprayed over FILL and falls through air pulled up by a FAN; about 1% EVAPORATES and the rest returns about 6 C cooler. The heat leaves the site as water vapour: HEAT OUT.

# 2. The parts

COOLING TOWER        evaporative heat rejection: water cooled by air and evaporation
BASIN                collects the cooled water at the bottom
LOUVERS              let air in at the sides, keep water in
FILL                 corrugated PVC sheets that spread the water into thin films
SPRAY NOZZLES        distribute the warm water over the fill
DRIFT ELIMINATORS    chevron blades that catch droplets in the leaving air
FAN                  induced-draft fan on top, usually on a variable-speed drive
MAKE-UP WATER        replaces what evaporates and is drained
BLOWDOWN             water bled off to control dissolved minerals
HEAT OUT             the heat's final exit into the outdoor air

# 3. Through the tower, in order

1. warm water is pumped to the top
2. SPRAY NOZZLES spread it over the FILL
3. films on the fill meet rising air
4. some evaporates, carrying heat away
5. the cooled water rains into the BASIN
6. it is pumped back to the chiller's condenser

# 4. The physics: latent heat, range and approach

Evaporating 1 kg of water takes about 2,400 kJ, while cooling 1 kg by 6 C releases about 25 kJ, so ~1% evaporating cools the rest ~6 C. The water can approach but not reach the air's wet-bulb temperature. RANGE is hot-in minus cold-out (~5-6 C); APPROACH is cold-out minus wet-bulb (typically 3-5 C). A closer approach needs a bigger tower.

# 5. Fill and air

FILL turns the water into thin films with a huge wet surface. Counterflow towers send air straight up against the falling water; crossflow towers draw it in from the sides. Induced-draft FANs on top, often several metres across and on variable-speed drives, set the airflow.

# 6. Water use

About 1% of the flow evaporates per ~6 C of cooling: roughly 1.5 L per kWh of heat rejected, plus BLOWDOWN. 100 MW of heat evaporates about 3.6 million litres a day; a 1 GW campus cooled this way would use about ten times that. Towers typically run at 3-6 cycles of concentration.

# 7. Drift, blowdown and safety

DRIFT ELIMINATORS hold droplet loss to about 0.0005-0.001% of circulating flow. BLOWDOWN keeps minerals from causing scale and corrosion. Tower water is treated with biocides and inhibitors and managed under water-management plans such as ASHRAE Standard 188 to control Legionella.

# 8. Who makes it

SPX Technologies (SPXC) makes Marley towers; EVAPCO and Baltimore Aircoil (Amsted) are large private makers. Johnson Controls (JCI) and Trane (TT) make the water-cooled chillers beside them; Modine (MOD) sells dry coolers, the waterless alternative; Vertiv (VRT) covers the thermal chain to the rack. Market shares are not sourced here.

# 9. HEAT OUT: chokepoints and risks

- Every watt a campus draws ends as heat in the outdoor air; wet towers are the cheapest way to put it there.
- Water use draws pushback, permit fights and siting limits in dry and water-stressed regions.
- Dry coolers use no water but more fan power and leave warmer water; warm-water liquid cooling makes them viable most of the year.
