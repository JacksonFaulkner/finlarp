# 1. What happens: a bit is charge on a capacitor

A DRAM bit is a packet of charge on one tiny capacitor. Full charge reads as 1, empty reads as 0. The cell is one transistor and one capacitor, so it is called 1T1C. The picture for this node is the animation above; the parts are below.

# 2. The parts

word line            row select, buried in a trench under a nitride cap; high turns on every
                     access transistor in its row
access transistor    a switch between the bit line and the storage node; the gate pulls a
                     U-shaped channel round the trench bottom, and charge flows through that
                     channel (never through the gate oxide)
storage capacitor    a tall cylinder: TiN electrode, high-k dielectric (ZrO2 stacks), TiN plate,
                     on a landing pad and storage-node contact; holds the bit as charge
bit line             the column wire, across the word lines; one cell at each crossing
sense amp            two cross-coupled inverters between BL and its twin BL-bar; latches to a
                     full rail
BL-bar               the twin line from the neighbouring mat (open bit line); its cells are not
                     read, so it stays at VDD/2 as the reference
precharge            the equaliser shorts BL to BL-bar, holding both at VDD/2 between accesses

# 3. One read, in order

1. precharge: the bit line sits at VDD/2
2. word line rises: the access transistor switches on
3. charge sharing: charge moves between the cell and the bit line; the bit line moves a small amount (tens of mV), up for a 1, down for a 0; BL-bar stays at VDD/2
4. sense amp: compares BL with BL-bar, then drives BL to a full VDD or GND and BL-bar the other way
5. restore: the still-connected cell is driven back to its full charge

The read drains the cell, so the restore step is part of every read, not an extra.

# 4. Refresh: rewrite every row before the charge is gone

The cell leaks, so every row is read and rewritten on a timer. In DDR4 the refresh window is 64 ms. The controller issues 8192 refresh commands in each window, and each one refreshes a slice of rows, so every row gets its turn.

# 5. Leakage: why the charge fades

Charge leaves the storage node four ways:

subthreshold         through the access transistor while it is off, out the drain to the bit line
junction             from the source n+ into the p-type substrate (a reverse-biased diode)
GIDL                 gate-induced drain leakage, pulled out by the strong field at the gate edge
dielectric           tunnelling through the thin capacitor dielectric

Junction leakage and GIDL grow with temperature, so hot DRAM refreshes twice as often (32 ms above 85 C). The loss is slow and differs from cell to cell, so the refresh window has to cover the weakest cells.

# 6. By the numbers

cell                 1 transistor + 1 capacitor (1T1C)
cell area            6F^2 with an open bit line, 8F^2 with a folded bit line
refresh window       tREFW = 64 ms (DDR4, Micron datasheet value)
refresh interval     tREFI = 7800 ns, 8192 REF commands per window
typical array        512 wordlines x 512 bitlines

# 7. Shrinking the cell: less area means less signal

The cell area is set by F, the minimum feature size (see the LITHO node). The swing on the bit line is (Vcell - VDD/2) x Cc / (Cc + Cb). A smaller storage capacitor gives a smaller swing, so the sense amp has less to work with. Designs keep Cc up with a taller capacitor or a better dielectric. Today's capacitors are about 40 times taller than wide and lean on a silicon-nitride support lattice.

The next steps change the cell's shape rather than its size. 4F2 stands the transistor up: a vertical silicon pillar with the gate wrapped round it, the bit line buried below and the capacitor on top, a 2F x 2F cell. 3D DRAM lays each cell on its side and stacks dozens of layers inside one die, like NAND, with the bit line running vertically through them. That is different from HBM, which stacks whole finished dies with TSVs. See the R&D 3D DRAM page.

# 8. Who makes it

Commodity DRAM is made by Micron (MU), SK hynix (000660.KS) and Samsung (005930.KS). Market share and the split between commodity and HBM are covered on the market pages, not here.

# 9. Chokepoints and risks

- Retention varies from cell to cell. The refresh window has to cover the weakest cells, which costs refresh time and power.
- Refresh blocks memory access while it runs, so it slows the system.
- Rapid activation of one row can leak charge into its neighbours (RowHammer), a reliability and security risk.
