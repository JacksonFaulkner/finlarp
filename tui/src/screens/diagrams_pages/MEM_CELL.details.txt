== word line
# What it does
The word line runs across a row of the array and drives the gate of every access transistor in that row. Raising it turns those transistors on, which connects each cell in the row to its bit line at once.
# Why it matters
Only one word line is high at a time, so one row is selected per access. Row selection is what makes the array fast: the whole row is sensed in parallel.
# Source
Jacob, ENEE 359a DRAM Systems, slide 3; Jacob, DRAM Circuits, slide 5 (word line driver, 512x512 organization).

== access transistor
# What it does
The access transistor is a switch between the bit line and the storage node. Its gate is the word line, so the row decides which cells are connected to their bit lines.
# Why it matters
It is the only transistor in the 1T1C cell, which is why the cell is small. Its leakage when off also sets how long the stored charge lasts.
# Source
Jacob, ENEE 359a DRAM Systems, slide 3 (switching element is a transistor); HiRA, arXiv 2209.10198, p. 2.

== storage capacitor
# What it does
The storage capacitor holds the bit as charge on its plate. Full charge reads as 1 and empty reads as 0. It is the only place the data lives.
# Why it matters
Charge leaks out of this capacitor, so the bit is not stable without refresh. The capacitance also sets the swing the sense amp sees.
# Source
Jacob, ENEE 359a DRAM Systems, slide 3 (storage capacitor); Jacob, DRAM Circuits, slide 9 (cell area 6F^2).

== bit line
# What it does
The bit line is the column wire. Each cell in a column connects to it through its access transistor. Before a read, the bit line is precharged to VDD/2.
# Why it matters
The cell is tiny, so its charge moves a much bigger bit line only a little. The sense amp has to detect that small move.
# Source
Jacob, DRAM Circuits, slides 4 and 11 (charge sharing and precharge to VDD/2); HiRA, arXiv 2209.10198, p. 2.

== precharge
# What it does
Precharge holds the bit line at VDD/2 between accesses. It is switched on when the word line falls, so the next read starts from the middle of the swing.
# Why it matters
A read moves the bit line up or down from VDD/2. If the line started anywhere else, a 1 and a 0 would not look different enough for the sense amp.
# Source
Jacob, DRAM Circuits, slides 4 and 11 (precharge to Vcc/2 before a read).

== charge sharing
# What it does
Charge sharing is what happens when the access transistor opens: the cell capacitor and the precharged bit line exchange charge. The bit line moves by a small voltage, up for a 1 and down for a 0.
# Why it matters
The move is small because the bit line capacitance is much bigger than the cell capacitance. The sense amp must amplify this small change.
# Source
Jacob, DRAM Circuits, slides 4 and 11: final voltage (Cb VBL + Cc Vcell) / (Cb + Cc). The swing follows from that: (Vcell - VDD/2) Cc / (Cc + Cb), derived from charge conservation.

== sense amp
# What it does
The sense amp is a pair of cross-coupled inverters on each bit line pair. It latches the small difference in the two lines to full VDD or full GND, then drives the cell back to its full charge.
# Why it matters
This latch does two jobs: it reads the bit and it restores the destroyed charge. Without it, a read would lose the data.
# Source
Jacob, DRAM Circuits, slides 12-14 (sense amplifier circuit and timing); HiRA, arXiv 2209.10198, p. 2 (sense amplifiers).

== restore
# What it does
Restore writes the full charge back into the cell after the read. The read drained the capacitor, and the sense amp drives the bit line to a full rail, which charges the cell back through the open access transistor.
# Why it matters
Without restore, every read would destroy the bit it reads. Restore is what makes a destructive read safe: the bit is written back right after it is read. It is the same write-back that refresh uses.
# Source
Jacob, DRAM Circuits, slides 12-14 (sense and restore); HiRA, arXiv 2209.10198, p. 2.

== refresh
# What it does
Refresh rewrites each row before its charge leaks away. Refresh is a read followed by a write-back, done by the controller for every row on a timer.
# Why it matters
Refresh is needed because the bit fades. In DDR4 the window is 64 ms; the controller issues 8192 refresh commands per window, one every 7.8 us.
# Source
PROTEAS, arXiv 2404.16256, Table I (Micron DDR4 datasheet: tREFW 64 ms, tREFI 7800 ns, 8192 REFs per window).

== leakage
# What it does
Leakage is the slow loss of charge from the storage node while the cell sits idle. It runs through the capacitor dielectric, the storage-node junction and the off access transistor.
# Why it matters
Leakage sets the refresh window. Cells differ in how fast they leak, and the window must cover the weakest cell on the chip.
# Source
Mutlu, Retrospective: Data Retention Behavior in Modern DRAM (arXiv 2306.16037, p. 1) for retention variation. The split between the three leak paths is not sourced here.
