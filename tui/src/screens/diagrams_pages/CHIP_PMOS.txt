# 1. What happens: a hole-carrying transistor, paired with an NMOS

A PMOS is the mirror image of an NMOS: p+ source and drain in an n-type well, switched on by pulling the gate low, carrying holes instead of electrons. Holes are the weaker carrier (HOLE MOBILITY), so every PMOS trick exists to close the gap: a SiGe STRESSOR squeezes the channel, BORON DOPING makes the p+ regions, and the PMOS GATE is the same high-k metal gate as the NMOS. Paired with an NMOS it makes the INVERTER at the heart of CMOS.

# 2. The parts

p+ SOURCE        SiGe:B stressor on the Vdd side
PMOS CHANNEL     holes flow source to drain when the gate is low
p+ DRAIN         SiGe:B stressor, the output
PMOS GATE        metal gate on HfO2 with spacers
N-WELL           n-type silicon tied to Vdd through an n+ tap; reverse-biases the p+/n junctions
SiGe STRESSOR    the source and drain regrown as SiGe, which is bigger than silicon and squeezes the channel
BORON DOPING     boron grown into the epitaxy in place; it diffuses fast, a risk to the channel

# 3. CMOS: pairing with an NMOS

In an INVERTER the PMOS sits on top (pull-up to Vdd) and the NMOS PAIR below (pull-down to ground), gates tied and drains tied. Input 0: PMOS on, NMOS off, output charged to Vdd. Input 1: PMOS off, NMOS on, output discharged to ground. There is never a Vdd-to-ground path except briefly mid-flip, so only switching draws power (CMOS POWER: C x V^2 x f, plus a small short-circuit term).

# 4. Standard cell layout

Logic is tiled from STD CELL layouts: a PMOS strip in the n-well on top, an NMOS strip below, a shared gate running vertically, with Vdd and ground rails along the edges. TAP CELL layouts tie the wells to their rails with n+ and p+ taps, preventing latch-up. Cell height is counted in metal-2 tracks: ASAP7 has 9-track and 7.5-track cells; 7-8 tracks favour area, 11-12 favour speed.

# 5. NMOS vs PMOS balance

HOLE MOBILITY in silicon is about 450 cm2/Vs against 1400 for electrons, so equal drive needs mu_n x Wn = mu_p x Wp: the WIDTH RATIO Wp/Wn is about 2-2.5 for equal rise and fall. Strain narrows the gap, so modern ratios shrink. In FinFET and GAA the width comes in whole fins or sheets, so the PMOS gets more fins or wider sheets.

# 6. Strain and doping

PMOS wants compressive strain along the channel (SiGe source/drain); NMOS wants tensile (nitride cap, SiC or SiP source/drain). Intel's 90 nm strain gave PMOS +25% drive and NMOS +10% for about 2% added process cost; Intel reported 0.72 mA/um PMOS drive at 1.2 V in 2004. Applied Materials claimed over 65% drive gain at 65 nm from its epi (vendor claim).

# 7. Germanium channels and GAA PMOS

GE CHANNEL: germanium's hole mobility is about 1900 against silicon's 450. GAA PFET: IBM's SiGe nanosheet pFET, at about 1 GPa strain, showed +100% peak hole mobility, -40% channel resistance and swing under 70 mV/decade. A patent describes graded SiGe source/drain at 55-60% Ge to even out strain across stacked sheets. The hard parts: germanium oxide quality, junction leakage and boron diffusing into the channel.

# 8. Who makes it

TSMC (TSM), Intel (INTC) and Samsung build PMOS stressors into every FinFET and GAA node. Applied Materials (AMAT) makes epi tools (53% of the epi market in 2004 per Gartner); ASM International and Kokusai make epi and furnace tools. Synopsys (SNPS) and Cadence (CDNS) do layout, place-and-route and cell characterisation; Arm (ARM) sells cell libraries. NVIDIA and AMD are fabless and take whatever PMOS drive the foundry gives.

# 9. Chokepoints and risks

- Holes stay the weaker carrier: every node needs new strain or materials to keep PMOS up.
- Boron diffusion and germanium oxide quality limit Ge-rich channels.
- Epitaxy tools come from few vendors; current epi market shares are not public in the sources found.
