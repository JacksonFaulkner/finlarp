# 1. What happens: AC in at the top, DC down the back

Power cables from the PDU plug into POWER SHELF units at the top and bottom of the rack. Each shelf holds six PSU modules that convert AC to about 50 V DC and push it onto a copper BUSBAR running down the back of the rack. Every compute tray and switch tray clips onto the busbar and takes its share. A GB200 NVL72 rack draws about 120 kW this way.

# 2. The parts

PDU                 power distribution unit: breakers and outlets feeding the rack
POWER SHELF         a 1U shelf of power supplies, 33 kW in an NVL72
PSU                 one power-supply module, 5.5 kW, AC to DC
BUSBAR              a copper bar down the back carrying DC to every tray
48 V                the rack DC bus family (about 50 V nominal in NVL72)
KILOWATTS PER RACK  about 120 kW for NVL72, 132 kW design power, peaks near 192 kW
800 VDC             NVIDIA's plan to feed 1 MW racks with 800 V DC from 2027
SIDECAR             a separate power rack beside the compute rack

# 3. Power through the rack, in order

1. 415/480 V three-phase AC arrives from the PDU
2. each POWER SHELF splits it across six PSU modules
3. the PSUs make about 50 V DC
4. the BUSBAR carries it down the back of the rack
5. each tray takes DC through a clip and converts it again on the board (see VRM)

# 4. Why so many amps

Power is voltage times current: 120 kW at about 50 V is about 2,400 A, and a 192 kW peak is over 3,800 A. That is why the BUSBAR is a thick copper bar, not a cable, and why every connector in the path is rated for hundreds of amps.

# 5. Shelves and redundancy

NVIDIA's DGX GB200 guide describes eight 33 kW shelves for N+N redundancy; other builds use six. Each shelf's six 5.5 kW PSU modules can be pulled and replaced while the rack runs.

# 6. KILOWATTS PER RACK keeps rising

Cloud racks drew 10-20 kW. GB200 NVL72 is about 120 kW (HPE lists 132 kW design power, peaks near 192 kW), and NVIDIA's next racks aim at 600 kW to 1 MW. At 1 MW, a 54 V bus would need up to 200 kg of copper busbar per rack, according to NVIDIA.

# 7. 800 V DC

NVIDIA's 800 VDC plan converts 13.8 kV AC straight to 800 V DC at the edge of the building and runs it to the racks, removing conversion steps; it claims up to 30% lower total cost of ownership, with full-scale production alongside its Kyber racks in 2027. Higher voltage means sixteen times less current than 50 V, so far thinner copper. A SIDECAR power rack can do the final conversion beside the compute rack.

# 8. Who makes it

Power shelves and PSUs: Delta, Lite-On, Vertiv (VRT), Flex. PDUs and busway: Vertiv, Schneider, Eaton (ETN), Legrand. NVIDIA (NVDA) sets the rack reference designs; around 20 partners support 800 VDC.

# 9. Chokepoints and risks

- Thousands of amps per rack strain connectors and busbars.
- 800 V DC needs new safety practice, breakers and connectors.
- Each rack's power spikes as GPUs sync, pushing designs to add in-rack storage.
