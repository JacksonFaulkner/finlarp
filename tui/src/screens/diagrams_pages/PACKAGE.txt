# 1. What happens: a die becomes a package

Packaging takes a tested die, attaches it to a substrate, connects it with wires or bumps, seals it, and provides the power and signal paths a board needs. For an AI GPU the package also holds HBM memory stacks beside the logic die, so this is the step where logic and memory are joined. Only dies that passed WAFER SORT and DICE reach this step.

# 2. Die attach: wire bond or flip chip

Wire bonding is the older method. The die is glued face-up with adhesive or epoxy, and fine wires run from its bond pads to the substrate or leadframe. One vendor source puts wire bonding at roughly 75-80% of all IC interconnections worldwide, because it is cheap and suits most package types.

Flip chip turns the die face-down onto solder or copper bumps, so every connection forms at once. Flip chip packages need underfill, an epoxy that fills the gap under the die, to survive thermal cycling and drop shock.

# 3. Mold and substrate

Transfer molding encapsulates the die and wires in epoxy molding compound, one vendor source gives 175 C at 5-10 MPa, to protect them mechanically. The die sits on an organic substrate: a multi-layer board built up in thin dielectric layers. A conventional substrate is symmetric, with more than 10 ABF build-up layers on each side of the core. Adding layers unevenly to one side causes warpage, and large substrates get harder to keep flat as their size grows.

# 4. HBM: DRAM dies stacked on TSVs

An HBM stack is thinned DRAM dies stacked on a base logic die. Through-silicon vias (TSVs, copper columns that pass through each thinned die) carry signals and power up the stack, and microbumps join each layer. SK hynix says its 12-high HBM is in production and 16-high is under qualification (ServeTheHome, August 2026). Stacks are bonded with thermocompression plus non-conductive film (TC-NCF) or mass reflow molded underfill (MR-MUF). Hybrid bonding, which joins copper to copper, is the next step: it pushes bump pitch below 18 um, which MR-MUF cannot reach. An HBM4 part reported by the same source has over 20,000 TSVs and 16,148 base microbumps.

# 5. CoWoS: GPU and HBM on one silicon interposer

CoWoS (Chip-on-Wafer-on-Substrate) is TSMC's 2.5D packaging. GPU dies and HBM stacks sit side by side on a silicon interposer, a slab of silicon with fine wires and TSVs that carries the signals between them. The interposer sits on an organic substrate that fans the signals out to the board. CoWoS-S interposers reach up to about 3.3 times the reticle, roughly 2,700 mm2 (TSMC). Beyond that, TSMC points to CoWoS-L or CoWoS-R. A B200 package carries two reticle-sized GPU dies and eight HBM3E stacks, joined through the interposer.

# 6. Why packaging is the bottleneck

Every die and HBM stack placed on an interposer is expensive, so one bad part can ruin the whole package. Known-good stacked-die testing keeps defective memory from using costly interposer area. The interposer is itself made on a wafer, and bigger interposers and bigger substrates are harder to keep flat. Counterpoint Research, in an older report, named CoWoS as the main bottleneck for AI shipments. As stacks climb past 20 dies, heat rather than bonding becomes the limit on height (ServeTheHome).

# 7. The parts on the page

INTERPOSER is the passive silicon slab that carries the wiring between dies. CHIP-ON-WAFER is the step that places known-good GPU and HBM dies on the interposer wafer, and FLIP ONTO sets that chip-plus-interposer module on the organic substrate. GPU DIE A is one of the two compute dies in a B200 package, and [3] HBM is the memory stack beside it. [4] microbumps and [8] C4 bumps carry the signals at two pitches, and [6] TSV are the copper-filled vias that carry them through silicon. [7] NV-HBI is the link that joins the two GPU dies, and [9] ORGANIC SUBSTRATE fans the signals out toward the board.

MOLD + is the finishing step: the mould, the second underfill, the [1] LID and cold plate, the [2] TIM between the die backs and the lid, and the BGA balls under the substrate.

CoWoS-L is the route past the reticle limit: local silicon bridges and redistribution wiring replace one full interposer. The supply gap is the shortfall between CoWoS demand and capacity, which TrendForce's June 2026 estimate puts near 20% now. ASE and Amkor take overflow back-end work while CoWoS is short.
