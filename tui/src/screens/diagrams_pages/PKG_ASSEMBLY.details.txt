== CHIP ON WAFER
# What it does
Known-good logic and HBM dies are bonded face down onto a whole, uncut interposer wafer, underfilled, and the wafer is thinned to expose its TSVs.
# Why it matters
Working at wafer scale is precise and efficient, but every die placed must be good.
# Source
TSMC 3DFabric; textbook flow.

== ON SUBSTRATE
# What it does
The interposer wafer is cut, and each interposer with its dies is bonded to an organic substrate through C4 reflow.
# Why it matters
This step joins the fine-pitch world to the board-pitch world.
# Source
TSMC 3DFabric; textbook flow.

== REFLOW
# What it does
Reflow heats the assembly until the solder melts and forms joints, then cools it.
# Why it matters
Heating and cooling warp the substrate and stress every joint, so profiles are tightly controlled.
# Source
Textbook.

== UNDERFILL FLOW
# What it does
Epoxy is dispensed at the die edge, wicks under by capillary action, and is cured, at both the die and the interposer level.
# Why it matters
It protects the joints from thermal stress; voids in it are a failure source.
# Source
UCLA (Totta) underfill life.

== TEST
# What it does
Testing happens at wafer sort, after chip-on-wafer and on the finished package.
# Why it matters
A defect found late scraps the whole assembly with all its HBM, so testing early matters.
# Source
Textbook.

== YIELD STACKUP
# What it does
Package yield is the product of every die's and every step's yield: at 99% per part, ten parts give about 90%.
# Why it matters
More stacks and bigger packages make yield harder; known-good dies are essential.
# Source
Arithmetic; illustrative.

== CAPACITY
# What it does
CoWoS capacity is the number of interposer wafers packaged per month.
# Why it matters
It, not wafer supply, has limited high-end AI accelerator shipments; NVIDIA reportedly took 40-50% in 2024.
# Source
Tom's Hardware; TrendForce (reports).

== OSAT
# What it does
Outsourced assembly and test houses, such as ASE and Amkor, take parts of the flow; UMC supplies interposers.
# Why it matters
They add capacity beyond TSMC's own lines.
# Source
TrendForce.
