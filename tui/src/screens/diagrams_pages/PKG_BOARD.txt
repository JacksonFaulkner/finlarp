# 1. What happens: the package meets the board, its power and its cooling

The finished package solders to a board through BGA BALLS, usually on a MODULE that carries the GPU, its power stages and its connectors. POWER DELIVERY brings hundreds of amps at under a volt from the board's VRM up through the substrate, bumps and interposer into the die. All of that power leaves as heat through a lid into a COLD PLATE, and at AI densities LIQUID COOLING replaces air. NVLINK and similar links tie GPUs together, and many modules fill a RACK.

# 2. The parts

BGA BALLS        solder balls joining the substrate to the board, at about 1 mm pitch
MODULE           the board carrying the GPU package, power stages and connectors
POWER DELIVERY   the path from regulator to transistor, losing voltage at every step
VRM              voltage regulators on the board, as close to the package as possible
COLD PLATE       a copper block with fins or channels pressed onto the lid
LIQUID COOLING   coolant pumped through cold plates instead of air
NVLINK           NVIDIA's GPU-to-GPU link (other vendors have their own)
RACK             many modules, power shelves and switches in one cabinet

# 3. Power in, heat out, in order

1. the VRM steps board power down to under a volt
2. current climbs through BGA, substrate, C4, interposer, microbumps
3. the logic die turns it into compute and heat
4. heat crosses the lid into the cold plate
5. coolant carries it away to the facility

# 4. Power delivery

Every level of the package drops voltage by I x R, and at hundreds of amps small resistances matter. VRMs sit right beside the package; backside power on the die (see TOP METAL) shortens the last step.

# 5. Cooling

The logic die is the main heat source. A lid spreads its heat into a COLD PLATE; at AI power densities, LIQUID COOLING through the plate replaces air, which also lets racks hold more GPUs. Future packages are expected to need thousands of watts (Tom's Hardware, TSMC roadmap coverage).

# 6. Modules and links

A MODULE carries the package with its power stages; NVLINK lanes run from the package through the board to switches so many GPUs act as one. Package size growth means larger modules and boards.

# 7. Racks

Many modules, power shelves, network switches and cooling manifolds fill a RACK (see the Power and Network+Cool tabs).

# 8. Who makes it

NVIDIA (NVDA) and AMD design modules; board makers and ODMs assemble them; VRM, connector and cooling suppliers fill in (not sourced here).

# 9. Chokepoints and risks

- Power per package keeps rising; delivery losses rise with it.
- Air cooling runs out; liquid cooling needs new infrastructure.
- Larger packages mean larger boards and more warpage at assembly.
