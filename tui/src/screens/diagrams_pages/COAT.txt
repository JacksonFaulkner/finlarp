# 3. The physics and chemistry

  film thickness  t  falls roughly with the inverse square root of spin speed:

          t  ~  k * c * eta^(1/3) / sqrt(w)          w = rpm, c = solids content, eta = viscosity
          (Meyerhofer-type scaling; textbook form, constants fitted per resist)

  Measured example, AZ 4533 thick resist: 4.0 um at 2000 rpm, 3.3 um at 4000, 2.3 um at 6000.
  Conventional spin: 2000-4000 rpm for 20-30 s; stopping early makes thickness sensitive to spin time.

  resist = POLYMER (backbone) + PAG (photo-acid generator) + QUENCHER base + SOLVENT (PGMEA is common)
           the solvent is most of the liquid; after the bake the film is mostly polymer + PAG
  soft bake (PAB) typically 90-110 C, a few minutes on a hot plate in the textbook range;
  one TEL Mark 8 example baked 60 s at 110 C and used 1500-4000 rpm to reach a 280 nm film.
  Goals of the bake: drive off solvent, densify the film, improve adhesion, stabilise exposure response.

# 4. By the numbers

  spin speed      2000-4000 rpm conventional; 1500-4000 rpm in a TEL Mark 8 recipe
  spin time       20-30 s (university guide); soft bake 60 s at 110 C in the TEL example
  soft bake       90-110 C (patent text: 'typically'), few minutes on a hot plate
  film thickness  280 nm in the TEL example (DUV era); 1.8 um for AZ 1518 at 4000 rpm (thick, MEMS-class)
  EUV film        ~20 nm layer absorbs only about 10% of 13.5 nm light, 90% passes through
  consequence     thin film means few photons caught per pixel: shot noise, see EXPOSE and DEVELOP
  market          Tokyo Electron + SCREEN: roughly 88-91% of coater/developer sales (aggregators)
  (bake is 90-110 C; the acid generator in the resist survives it intact)

# 7. The track: where COAT sits in the line

  FOUP in ─▶ PRIME ─▶ COAT ─▶ SOFT BAKE ─▶ COOL ─▶ interface ─▶ SCANNER (see EXPOSE)
                                                       ▲                    │
                                                       │                    ▼
  FOUP out ◀ COOL ◀ DEVELOP ◀ POST-EXPOSURE BAKE ◀─────┴───────── exposed wafer returns

  The track is one machine with coat, bake and develop modules stacked around robot arms; the scanner
  sits at the interface. Wafers leave coat, go to EXPOSE, and come back for PEB and DEVELOP.
  (layout is a generic textbook sketch; real tracks stack modules vertically to save floor space)

# 8. What goes wrong

  striations / comets   dried particles or bubbles in the dispense line leave streaks
  edge bead             resist piles on the rim; the EBR solvent step trims it
  thickness drift       temperature or humidity shifts solvent evaporation rate, so film thickness moves
  thin-film penalty     at ~20 nm, a 1 nm error is 5% of the film (arithmetic)
  (this list is textbook process knowledge; no quantitative defect rates found in sources)
