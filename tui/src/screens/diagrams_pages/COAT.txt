# 1. What happens: resist goes on in the track

The coat step puts a thin liquid film of resist on the wafer inside a coater-developer track. The track is one machine that holds coating cups, hot plates and chill plates in a single line, and a robot arm moves the wafer between them. The order is PRIME+BARC, then the resist coat, then SOFT BAKE, then COOL. The wafer then leaves the track for the scanner through the interface block.

PRIME+BARC: the wafer gets an adhesion primer, and often a bottom anti-reflective layer, before any resist goes on.

# 2. Inside a spin-coat cup

The wafer sits on a CHUCK, held by vacuum. A NOZZLE moves over the centre and drops resist. The wafer spins; the film spreads out and the excess is flung off into the cup.

DISPENSE: a small pool of liquid resist lands on the centre of the wafer.
SPIN: centrifugal force spreads the liquid into a thin, even film.
EDGE BEAD: spinning leaves a thick ridge of resist at the rim. The EBR nozzle sprays solvent on the edge only and removes it.
EBR nozzle: edge-bead removal, solvent on the rim and nothing in the middle.

# 3. Soft bake and cool

SOFT BAKE: a hot plate drives off the solvent and firms the film. One MicroChem sheet uses 100 C for 10 minutes; an AZ 10XT reference uses 110 C for 3 minutes.
COOL: a chill plate brings the wafer back to a steady temperature before the interface buffer.

# 4. The resist itself

The resist is a liquid mix of a polymer, a light-sensitive acid generator and a solvent. The solvent is often PGMEA. Most of the liquid is solvent, and the bake leaves mostly polymer and acid generator on the wafer. An EUV film is thin, so it absorbs little light and must be very uniform.

# 5. Who makes the track

Tokyo Electron is generally reported as the largest maker of coater-developer tracks; market-research summaries put it well ahead of the rest, though their figures are not audited. Its ACT-8 track runs in line with the ASML scanner in the EPFL example. SCREEN Holdings is the other major maker of coater-developers. SEMES, a Samsung affiliate, also builds tracks.

# 6. What goes wrong

striations / comets: dried particles or bubbles in the dispense line leave streaks on the film.
thickness drift: a change in temperature or humidity alters the solvent evaporation rate, so the film thickness moves.
