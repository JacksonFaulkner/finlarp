# 1. What happens: build one wiring level by dual damascene, repeated for M5-M9

The intermediate levels route signals block to block. Each pass makes one metal level plus the vias below it: DEPOSIT ILD (a low-k stack with an etch stop), PATTERN+ETCH (trench and via through a hard mask), BARRIER+SEED (TaN/Ta, Ru, by PVD, ALD or CVD), PLATE Cu (bottom-up electroplating), CMP (strip the excess, stop on the dielectric), CAP+REPEAT (a SiCN/SiN cap, then the next level). M5-M9 is five such passes, each about 4-6 tool visits.

# 2. Dual damascene in section

Trench and via are filled with copper in one plating step, so each level has one fill and one CMP. The barrier/liner coats the walls but not the via bottom, so via copper touches line copper directly. A SiCN or SiN cap separates levels; because the cap has higher k, thinner caps and selective metal caps are used to cut capacitance. Ultra-low-k dielectric sits between lines.

# 3. RC delay: why levels get fatter going up

Wire delay is about R x C = rho k eps0 L^2 / (W S): resistance falls with width and height, capacitance is mostly line to line and grows with k and with height over spacing. Thin M1-M4 have the highest resistance per micron (the size effect); medium M5-M9 balance R and C; thick M10-M14 carry power, clock and global wires at the lowest resistance. Copper resistivity rises as lines narrow: about 2.0 uOhm-cm in wide lines, over 3.0 below about 60 nm width (IBM). RC DELAY is the problem this whole stack manages.

# 5. The dielectric: lower k, softer film

LOW-K / ULK trades k against stiffness (Young's modulus): SiO2 (k about 4.0) 72 GPa; carbon-doped oxide (k about 2.7) 7-10 GPa; an Intel patent target (k 2.5 or less) 15 GPa; 58% porous CDO 3 GPa; spin-on k below 2.0, 1-3 GPa. Modulus falls an order of magnitude as k drops by a third, and films below k 2 are at least 50% porous. AIR GAP: removing the dielectric altogether cuts capacitance 25% or more.

# 6. Electromigration: how a copper line fails

ELECTROMIGRATION: electrons push copper atoms along the weakest path, the copper/cap interface, leaving voids upstream and hillocks downstream. Black's equation: MTTF = A x J^-n x exp(Ea / kT), n often about 2. Copper's activation energy is about 0.7-1.1 eV against aluminium's 0.5-0.7. Fixes: CoWP, Co or Ru caps, Mn or Co liners, copper reflow.

# 7. Vias and edge placement

VIA / EPE: a via landed where planned has low resistance; a misaligned via narrows at the bottom, raising resistance and shorting or electromigration risk. The overlay budget is about a quarter of the pitch at 70 nm and below; self-aligned vias set the via edge by the line grating. RU / SUBTRACTIVE: below about 20 nm pitch, etched ruthenium without a barrier replaces damascene copper.

# 8. Who makes it

Deposition, etch, CMP and plating tools: Applied Materials (AMAT: Endura CuBS barrier/seed with Ru CVD, Black Diamond low-k), Lam Research (LRCX: SABRE copper plating), Tokyo Electron (8035.T), Ebara (6361.T). Fabs: TSMC (TSM), Intel (INTC), Samsung (005930.KS). EDA RC EXTRACT: Synopsys StarRC (SNPS), Cadence Quantus (CDNS). Applied's FY2025 revenue was $28,368M, 30% ($8,529M) from China.

# 9. Chokepoints and risks

- A copper resistance wall below about 20 nm pitch (the switch to Ru).
- Damage to porous ultra-low-k films.
- Electromigration at higher current.
- China at 30% of Applied's sales, and tight levels moving to subtractive Ru, which changes who sells the tools.
