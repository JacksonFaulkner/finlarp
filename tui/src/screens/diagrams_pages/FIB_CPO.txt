# 1. What happens: the optics move onto the switch package

In a normal switch, the switch chip drives electrical signals across the board to pluggable modules at the FRONT PANEL, tens of centimetres away, and each module needs a DSP to clean them up. CO-PACKAGED OPTICS put an OPTICAL ENGINE (a silicon-photonics chip with its driver chip on top) on the switch package, millimetres from the chip. Light leaves the package in fiber. An EXTERNAL LASER in a pluggable box at the panel supplies the steady light.

# 2. The parts

CO-PACKAGED OPTICS   optical engines on the same package as the switch chip
OPTICAL ENGINE       a photonic chip (modulators, photodiodes) with an electronic chip stacked on it
MICRO-RING           a ring-shaped modulator a few microns across, tuned to one wavelength
EXTERNAL LASER       a pluggable laser source at the panel that feeds the engines through fiber
FRONT PANEL          where pluggables sit today and the external lasers sit with CPO
POWER PER PORT       watts per 1.6T port; the main reason for CPO
SERVICEABILITY       swapping a failed part: easy for a pluggable, hard for a package
COUPE                TSMC's process that stacks the electronic chip on the photonic chip

# 3. A bit's path with CPO, in order

1. the switch chip's SerDes drives a few millimetres of package wiring
2. the engine's electronic chip drives a MICRO-RING modulator
3. the ring imprints the data onto light from the EXTERNAL LASER
4. the light leaves the package in fiber to the FRONT PANEL
5. on receive, germanium photodiodes and TIAs in the engine turn light back into lanes

# 4. Why: the copper is the problem

At 200G per lane, a long trace from the switch chip to the FRONT PANEL loses so much signal that every module needs a DSP to recover it. Shortening the electrical path to millimetres lets the engine drive the optics directly. NVIDIA says its photonics switches cut power 3.5x versus pluggable transceivers; one report puts a 1.6T port at about 9 W instead of 25 W.

# 5. Inside the optical engine

NVIDIA's engines use 200 Gb/s MICRO-RING modulators made on TSMC's COUPE process, which stacks an electronic chip on a photonic one. A ring a few microns across couples light only at its resonant wavelength; a voltage shifts that resonance, turning the light up or down. Rings are tiny and efficient but sensitive to temperature, so each needs heater control.

# 6. Lasers stay pluggable

Lasers fail more often than silicon and run best cool, so CPO keeps them out at the panel in EXTERNAL LASER modules. Coverage of NVIDIA's Hot Chips 2025 talk says a 1.6 Tb/s link needs two lasers instead of eight, a 4x reduction.

# 7. The switches

NVIDIA's Spectrum-X and Quantum-X Photonics switches are scheduled from the second half of 2026, in configurations such as 128 ports of 800 Gb/s or 512 ports of 200 Gb/s, about 100 Tb/s in total. Broadcom also builds CPO versions of its Tomahawk switch chips.

# 8. Who makes it

NVIDIA (NVDA) and Broadcom (AVGO) design CPO switches; TSMC (TSM) makes the photonic and electronic chips and packages them; laser makers such as Lumentum (LITE) and Coherent (COHR) supply the external lasers; Corning (GLW) and others supply fiber and connectors.

# 9. Chokepoints and risks

- SERVICEABILITY: a failed engine means replacing the whole switch board, not one module.
- Attaching fibers to a package at scale, with low loss, is a new manufacturing step.
- It ties optics to one switch vendor's package instead of an open module market.
