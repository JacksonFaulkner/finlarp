# 3. The route from fab to data centre

  1 GPU module     B200/GB200 packages come from CoWoS, then final test (see PACKAGE, FINAL TEST).
  2 board + tray   an ODM mounts GB200 Bianca boards, NICs, cold plates and power into 1U trays.
  3 rack           trays, NVLink switch trays, power shelves, manifolds and cable spine go in.
  4 liquid test    loop is leak-checked and run at load before shipping (textbook).
  5 deliver        crated racks go by air and truck to a site with CDUs and 120 kW+ per rack ready.
  Roles:
    NVIDIA (NVDA)    sells GPU, board design, NVLink switches, reference design
    ODM (Foxconn, Quanta, Wistron, Wiwynn, Inventec)  builds to order, often to a cloud customer design
    OEM (Dell, HPE, Supermicro)  sells branded systems to enterprises, neoclouds and sovereigns
    Hyperscaler      Microsoft, Meta, AWS, Google buy racks or whole designs direct
  The chokepoints on the path: CoWoS output, HBM supply, then rack integration and data-centre power.
  TrendForce: Foxconn mostly assembles NVL72; Quanta is main assembler for NVL36.
  Wistron's GB200 shipments in 2Q25 mainly served Dell (KGI).
  Why racks, not servers: NVLink needs 72 GPUs wired at copper-reach, so the unit of sale is the whole
      rack.
  (rack as the unit of sale is Nvidia strategy per the NVL72 design; the reasoning is textbook)

  Supply-chain signals (secondary sources):
    Blackwell above 80% of Nvidia high-end GPU shipments in 2025 (TrendForce)
    Taiwan-to-US AI server shipments set to double in 2025 vs 2024 (Nikkei, via a blog summary)
    Hon Hai was the most aggressive ODM for GB200 shipment growth in 3Q25 guidance (KGI)
    Wiwynn is a key supplier for AWS Trainium systems; Quanta AI servers over 60% of server revenue
        mid-2025
    Final assembly is mostly Taiwan-based ODMs with plants in Taiwan, Mexico and US (location is
        textbook)

# 4. By the numbers

  GPUs per rack          72 Blackwell GPUs + 36 Grace CPUs
  power                  ~120 kW nominal; deployed 130-132 kW (Spheron). HPE spec: 132 kW TDP, peak
                         1.5x TDP, about 192 kW; HPE says provision 192 kW
  weight                 ~1.36 t (3,000 lb); HPE lists about 3,245 lb loaded
  NVLink                 130 TB/s all-to-all; NVLink-C2C 900 GB/s per Grace-Blackwell superchip
  memory                 13.4 TB HBM3e, 576 TB/s
  compute                1.44 exaflops FP4 with sparsity, 720 PFLOPS dense
  SemiAnalysis           123.6 kW total draw per NVL72 incl. AC-to-DC losses

# 8. Who makes it, market structure, risks

  ODMs   Foxconn / Hon Hai (2317.TW) widely put near 40% of general + AI server market (blog, unverified)
         Quanta (2382.TW): AI servers on track for 70% of its server revenue (CFO)
         Wistron (3231.TW) and Wiwynn (6669.TW): Wiwynn 2025 revenue NT$950.663bn; Wistron over
         NT$2.19tn, said to pass Quanta (LinkedIn posts; verify in filings)
         Top four (Foxconn, Quanta, Wistron, Inventec 2356.TW): 53.2% of server market, Q4 2025
         (aggregator citing Taipei Times, not confirmed)
  OEMs   Dell (DELL), HPE (HPE), Supermicro (SMCI)
  Power and cooling   Vertiv (VRT) and others (textbook, no share sourced)
  Risks  rack-level yield: 72 GPUs and the NVLink spine must all work or the rack is down;
         leaks in the liquid loop; 120-192 kW per rack outruns most existing halls;
         power and cooling can gate deployment more than chips; buyers are a handful of hyperscalers.
