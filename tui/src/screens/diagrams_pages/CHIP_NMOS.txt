# 1. What happens: a gate voltage opens an electron channel between two n+ islands

An NMOS transistor is a voltage-controlled switch. Two heavily doped n+ islands, the source and the drain, sit in p-type silicon. With the gate at zero (OFF STATE) there is no path between them. Raising the gate pushes holes away (DEPLETION), then pulls in a thin layer of electrons at the surface (INVERSION). With a small drain voltage that layer acts as a resistor (LINEAR); with a large one it pinches off at the drain and the current levels out (SATURATION). Dropping the gate back to zero empties the channel (TURN-OFF). No current flows into the gate: only its electric field reaches the silicon.

# 2. The parts

N+ SOURCE      arsenic or phosphorus, about 1e20-1e21 /cm3, usually grounded
GATE STACK     metal gate on high-k dielectric over a thin SiO2 layer, about 1 nm EOT class
N+ DRAIN       the output node, tied to the load or VDD
P-WELL         boron-doped body, about 1e15-1e18 /cm3; holes are the majority carrier
inside         halo pockets stop punch-through; spacers set the extension spacing; STI isolates neighbours

FinFET and gate-all-around devices wrap the same pieces around the channel (see the PLANAR, FINFET and GAA nodes).

# 3. One switching cycle, in order

1. OFF STATE: gate at 0 V; the junctions are reverse-biased and block current
2. DEPLETION: a small gate voltage pushes holes away from the surface
3. INVERSION: above the threshold voltage, electrons gather into a channel
4. LINEAR, then SATURATION: drain voltage drives the electrons; at high drain voltage the channel pinches off at the drain
5. TURN-OFF: the gate drops and the channel empties

# 4. Threshold and subthreshold: how sharply it turns on

Below the threshold voltage (SUBTHRESHOLD) the current still flows, rising exponentially with gate voltage: this is the off-state leakage. At the THRESHOLD the inversion layer forms and the current climbs steeply, then more slowly once the carriers reach their velocity limit.

swing      S = (kT/q) ln10 (1 + Cdep/Cox) >= 60 mV per decade at 300 K
real S     about 70-90 mV/decade, body factor m of 1.2-1.5
rule       each 60-100 mV of threshold reduction gives about 10x more off-state leakage, so Vt cannot be scaled freely

# 5. Short-channel effects and body bias

DIBL: as the channel shortens, the drain starts to lower the source barrier itself, so the threshold falls with drain voltage and leakage rises. BODY BIAS: the well voltage shifts the threshold (a square-root law), a knob for trading speed against leakage. FinFET and nanosheet gates fight short-channel effects by surrounding the channel so the gate, not the drain, owns the barrier.

# 6. Dopants and strain

ARSENIC diffuses slowly, giving abrupt, shallow extensions, but activates less fully. Phosphorus activates better but diffuses faster and deeper; it is used in the deep source/drain and as Si:P epitaxy. Si:P grown in place reaches about 1.2e21 /cm3 and 0.3 mOhm.cm and stretches the channel (tensile strain), which speeds electrons; contact resistivity down to 1.5e-9 Ohm.cm2 has been reported on 2e21 /cm3 Si:P.

# 8. Who makes it

TSMC (TSM) builds the transistors for NVIDIA, AMD and Apple; in 2025, 3 nm was 24% and 7 nm and below 74% of its wafer revenue (NT$3,809B, about US$122.4B). Intel (INTC) makes RibbonFET (GAA) NMOS and PMOS on 18A, with 4 NMOS and 4 PMOS threshold options over a 180 mV range; 18A-P adds a fifth pair and about +5% NMOS drive current. Synopsys (SNPS) and Cadence (CDNS) sell the device and circuit simulators that model these equations (FY25 revenue $7.054B and $5.297B). ARM licenses cores built from these switches.

# 9. Chokepoints and risks

- Leakage and threshold variability grow at 2 nm-class nodes.
- Strain and contact resistance limit drive current as devices shrink.
- The most advanced transistors come from very few fabs.
