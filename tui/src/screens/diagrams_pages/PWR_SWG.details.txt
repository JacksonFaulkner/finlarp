== MEDIUM VOLTAGE
# What it does
Medium voltage, 13.8 to 34.5 kV, is the level at which power is distributed around a campus from the substation to each building.
# Why it matters
It keeps cable sizes manageable over hundreds of metres; NVIDIA's 800 V DC plan converts straight from 13.8 kV at the perimeter.
# Source
NVIDIA 800 VDC blog; textbook.

== CIRCUIT BREAKER
# What it does
A circuit breaker opens a circuit on command or on a fault; at medium voltage the contacts separate inside a vacuum bottle so the arc dies quickly.
# Why it matters
It is what keeps one fault from taking down the whole site; breakers must be rated for the largest fault current the system can produce.
# Source
Textbook.

== UNIT SUBSTATION
# What it does
A unit substation pairs a transformer with its switchgear to step medium voltage down to 480 V (or 415 V) for a building or hall.
# Why it matters
Each one feeds a block of UPS and racks; as racks go from tens to hundreds of kilowatts, more and larger units are needed.
# Source
Textbook.

== 480 V
# What it does
480 V three-phase is the US low-voltage distribution level inside data centers; much of the world uses 415/240 V.
# Why it matters
It feeds UPS systems and rack power; at 480 V a 1 MW load draws about 1,200 A, so copper adds up fast.
# Source
Textbook.

== ATS
# What it does
An automatic transfer switch watches the utility supply and, after an outage, moves the load to the generators once they are running, then back again.
# Why it matters
It is the link between backup generators and the load; the UPS has to carry the load during the seconds the transfer takes.
# Source
NFPA 110; textbook.

== PROTECTION RELAY
# What it does
A protection relay measures currents and voltages and trips breakers when it detects a fault, coordinated so the nearest breaker opens first.
# Why it matters
Good coordination means a fault drops one feed, not the building.
# Source
Textbook (IEEE 242).

== ARC FLASH
# What it does
An arc flash is the explosive release of energy when current arcs through air in a fault, reaching thousands of degrees.
# Why it matters
It is the main safety hazard in power rooms; switchgear is built to contain arcs and work is done de-energized or in rated gear.
# Source
NFPA 70E.

== REDUNDANCY
# What it does
Redundancy duplicates power paths: 2N gives two complete independent paths, N+1 one spare unit per group.
# Why it matters
It lets any part fail or be serviced without dropping load, at the cost of buying and housing up to twice the equipment.
# Source
Textbook (Uptime tiers).
