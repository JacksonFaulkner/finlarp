# 1. What happens: tiny solder joints connect each die to the interposer

Every die in the package is joined to the layer below by an array of bumps. The coarsest, C4 BUMP solder balls at about 150 um pitch, join the interposer to the substrate. Under the dies, a MICROBUMP array at about 40 um pitch (each a CU PILLAR with a solder cap) joins logic and HBM to the interposer. Below about 10 um, HYBRID BOND joints replace solder with direct copper-to-copper contact. Smaller BUMP PITCH means more connections per mm2.

# 2. The parts

C4 BUMP           solder balls, about 150 um pitch, interposer or chip to substrate
MICROBUMP         about 40 um pitch: 20-25 um bumps with 15 um spacing
CU PILLAR         a plated copper post (about 25 um wide) with a nickel barrier and a tin-silver cap
IMC               intermetallic layers (Cu6Sn5, Ni3Sn4) where solder meets copper or nickel; they hold the joint
UBM               the seed and barrier metal under the pillar, over the die's aluminium pad
HYBRID BOND       copper-to-copper plus oxide-to-oxide, no solder, 10 um pitch and below
BUMP PITCH        centre-to-centre spacing; connection density goes with 1 / pitch squared
UNDERFILL         epoxy flowed between the layers to share the stress
ELECTROMIGRATION  current density wears out bumps as they shrink
SoIC              TSMC's hybrid-bonded die stacking

# 3. Making a joint, in order

1. copper pillars are electroplated on the die's pads while it is still a wafer, over a UBM seed
2. a nickel barrier and a tin-silver solder cap are plated on top
3. the die is flipped and placed face down, each pillar on its interposer pad
4. reflow (about 250 C) melts the cap into a joint and grows IMC at both ends; thermocompression bonding is used at the finest pitches
5. underfill is drawn into the gap by capillary action and cured

# 4. Bump pitch: from C4 to hybrid bonds

C4 about 150 um, microbump about 40 um, hybrid bond 10 um and below. Halving the pitch fits four times the connections in the same area. Microbumps are expected to hit a wall near 10 um, where solder bridges between neighbours.

# 5. Why hybrid bonding

HYBRID BOND joints bond polished copper pads directly while the surrounding oxide bonds too, with no solder and no gap, so pitch can fall to a few um and stacks get thinner. The steps: both faces are polished flat by CMP with the copper left dished a few nm; pressed together, the oxide bonds at room temperature; an anneal then expands the copper so the pads fuse. They demand very flat, clean surfaces: one particle leaves an unbonded void, which makes yield hard. TSMC's SoIC uses them for die-on-die stacking, and Amkor lists copper hybrid bonding as a development programme [5].

# 6. Underfill and stress

The die, interposer and substrate expand at different rates as they heat. UNDERFILL epoxy between them shares the stress across the whole area instead of concentrating it in the outer joints; for C4 joints it gives over 10x thermal-cycle life.

# 7. Current and electromigration

Power for the logic die enters through thousands of bumps. As bumps shrink, each carries more current density, and ELECTROMIGRATION pushes metal atoms along with the electron flow until a void opens, usually by the IMC where the current crowds into the solder. More bumps share the load, so power and ground take a large share of the bump sites under a big die.

# 8. Who makes it

TSMC (TSM) bumps and bonds dies in CoWoS and SoIC; ASE (ASX) and Amkor (AMKR) do bumping and flip-chip assembly; the HBM stacks, with microbumps between their DRAM layers, come from SK hynix, Micron and Samsung; Amkor is developing copper hybrid bonding [5]; Besi and others make hybrid-bonding tools (not sourced here).

# 9. Chokepoints and risks

- Solder bridging below about 10 um pitch forces the move to hybrid bonding.
- Hybrid bonding needs near-perfect flatness and cleanliness.
- Thermal stress cracks joints without good underfill.
