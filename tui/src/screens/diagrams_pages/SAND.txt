 ┌───────────┐   ┌───────┐   ┌───────────────┐   ┌────────────────┐   ┌───────────┐   ┌──────────────┐
 │ QUARTZITE │   │ CRUSH │   │ BLEND         │   │ ARC FURNACE    │   │ TAP       │   │ CAST + CRUSH │

# 2. Inside a submerged arc furnace

                 ELECTRODE 1        ELECTRODE 2        ELECTRODE 3
                      ║                  ║                  ║
                      ║                  ║                  ║      ◀── CO + SiO fume leaves the top
      ┌────────────────╨──────────────────╨──────────────────╨─────────┐   SiO re-oxidises to silica fume
      │  CHARGE: quartzite lumps + carbon, sinking slowly              │
      │                                                                │
      │    SiO2 + 3C ──▶ SiC + 2CO         (upper, cooler charge)      │
      │  ≈≈≈≈≈≈≈≈≈≈≈ ARC CAVITIES, above 1800 C ≈≈≈≈≈≈≈≈≈≈≈≈≈≈≈≈≈≈≈≈≈≈≈│
      │    SiO2 + C ──▶ SiO(gas) + CO     SiC + SiO ──▶ 2Si + CO       │
      │                                                                │
      │  ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓ MOLTEN SILICON POOL ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓          │
      └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┬───────┘
                                                               ▼  tap hole ──▶ ladle ──▶ casting bed

  (3 electrodes on 3-phase AC; arcs burn in cavities under the burden, which is the resistor)

# 3. The chemistry (overall vs what really happens)

  overall      SiO2 + 2C ──▶ Si + 2CO
  step 1       SiO2 + 3C ──▶ SiC + 2CO         makes silicon carbide in the cooler zone
  step 2       SiO2 +  C ──▶ SiO + CO          makes SiO gas in the hot zone
  step 3       SiC + SiO ──▶ 2Si + CO          textbook closing step, gives liquid Si

# 4. By the numbers

  temperature     > 1800 C in the arc zone
  electricity     11 to 13 MWh per tonne of silicon (one patent example: 11.2 MWh/t)
  trend           bigger furnaces + better control keep pushing MWh/t down
  output          98-99% pure Si, about 2N; roughly 10,000 ppm impurities

# 8. Chokepoint: high-purity quartz for crucibles

  Spruce Pine, North Carolina: est. 70-90% of commercial high-purity quartz (HPQ).
  Mines: Sibelco and The Quartz Corp (Imerys / Norsk Mineral JV). Crucibles used in MELT + PULL;
  last hundreds of hours, so an outage (Hurricane Helene, 2024) shakes wafer supply.
