 │ EXPOSED      │  │ PEB           │  │ COOL      │  │ DEVELOP        │  │ RINSE     │  │ TO ETCH     │

  (PEB = post-exposure bake. Until PEB and develop the wafer holds only a latent chemical image.)

# 2. The chemistry in a chemically amplified resist (CAR)

  EXPOSE   PAG + 92 eV photon ──▶ H+ (photoacid); EUV secondary electrons make extra acid

  PEB      polymer-COO-tBu + H+ ──▶ polymer-COOH + isobutene + H+      acid is regenerated: CATALYTIC
           (t-butyl ester shown as the textbook example; real resists mix several protecting groups)

  DEVELOP  polymer-COOH + OH- ──▶ polymer-COO- + H2O       carboxylate dissolves in aqueous TMAH
           developer: (CH3)4N+ OH-, 2.38 wt% (about 0.26 mol/L by arithmetic: 23.8 g/L / 91.15 g/mol)

  QUENCHER a weak base in the resist that neutralises stray acid, limiting how far it spreads
  (equations are textbook; the search confirmed t-butyl deprotection by photoacid and TMAH at 2.38 wt%)

# 4. The physics: acid diffusion sets the blur

  Gaussian blur of the acid:     sigma = sqrt(2 * D * t)      D = acid diffusivity, t = bake time
  One EUV study fitted D = ~25 nm2/s for a commercial polymer CAR (Gaussian kernel); it also found
  linewidth versus bake time was non-linear, so treat the numbers below as rough illustration only.

  Too little blur: more dose needed (low sensitivity). Too much: lost resolution, rough line edges.
  This is the CAR trade-off. EUV and e-beam exposure occur in vacuum, which can dry the film and weaken
  the acid-catalysed reaction in the later bake (reported). Fast lasers can bake in milliseconds:
  one laser PEB study reported acid diffusivity 100-1000x lower than hot-plate extrapolations and
  needed under half the EUV dose.

# 5b. By the numbers

  PEB               90-150 C for 30-120 s (CAR range, SPIE paper); 130 C used for a t-butyl ester resist
  developer         2.38 wt% TMAH, the standard aqueous developer (works for a negative-tone EUV resist)
  acid diffusivity  ~25 nm2/s in the fitted commercial CAR
  dose              30 mJ/cm2 reference, ~20 photons per nm2 (see EXPOSE, arithmetic)
  resist film       ~20 nm absorbs only ~10% of EUV light, so few photons make the image
  dry resist        Lam says Aether uses 5-10x less chemicals than wet resist processes (company claim)
  TMAH market       USD 1.16B in 2025 to 1.98B by 2032 (one report; others give USD 1.8B, estimates vary)

# 7. Where it goes wrong: stochastics and collapse

  Few photons mean some pixels get too few or too many: random bridges, broken lines, missing contacts.
  Chemically amplified resists: noise from polymer molecular-weight spread and uneven PAG / quencher mix.
  Metal-oxide resists (MOR) absorb about 3x more but have no acid-blur smoothing, so edges stay rough;
  strong top absorption can leave an undercut profile (simulation study). Raising CAR dose also thins the
  film after develop, so dose is no free fix. Tall thin lines can collapse in rinse + dry (textbook).
  Lam DRY RESIST (Aether): deposited and developed with no wet chemistry; an unnamed leading memory
  maker chose it for advanced DRAM (Jan 2025). Lam + JSR/Inpria signed a cross-licence in Sept 2025.
