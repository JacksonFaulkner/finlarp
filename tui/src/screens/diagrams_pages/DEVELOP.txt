# 1. What happens: the pattern appears

After exposure and the post-exposure bake, the resist holds only an invisible chemical image. Develop washes the soluble part away on the coater/developer track. In a positive resist the exposed resist dissolves; the unexposed resist stays and becomes the stencil for etch. The develop module sits on the same track as the bake plates and the cool plate, and the wafer moves between them by robot arm.

# 2. The bake and cool before develop

PEB: the post-exposure bake on the HOT PLATE lets photoacid cut the protecting groups in a chemically amplified resist, so each acid molecule cuts many groups. Too long a bake or too hot a plate lets the acid spread, which blurs the line edges.

COOL: the chill plate stops that reaction and brings the wafer to a steady temperature before develop.

Gaussian blur: the acid diffuses during the bake, and the blur width grows with the square root of the bake time (sigma = sqrt(2 D t)). The bake setting therefore sets line width and edge roughness.

# 3. Inside the develop cup

The wafer sits on a CHUCK, held by vacuum, inside a cup with a drain. A nozzle lays the developer on the wafer.

DEVELOP: aqueous TMAH, typically 2.38 wt%, is the standard developer for these resists. Puddle and immersion modes both use it; one SST study used a typical 60 s puddle. The developer dissolves the exposed positive resist and leaves the rest.

TMAH dissolves the exposed resist by turning its acid groups into soluble salts. The dissolved resist must then leave the wafer, or it can settle back onto it.

QUENCHER: a weak base in the resist that mops up stray acid. It sharpens the boundary between exposed and unexposed resist, at the cost of some sensitivity, since neutralised acid cuts no protecting groups.

# 4. Rinse, dry and the stencil

RINSE: deionized water washes away the developer and dissolved resist, then the wafer spins dry or is dried with nitrogen. As the liquid pulls back, tall thin lines can lean together and collapse, so line aspect ratio limits what the rinse can survive.

TO ETCH: the developed resist is the stencil. Etch carves the film below wherever the trenches are open, and the resist is stripped afterward. Any defect in the stencil is copied into the film.

# 5. The resist types and the defects

Metal-oxide resists absorb more EUV light and are being developed for finer pitches; their edges behave differently from chemically amplified resist. Dry resist is a separate route with no wet develop.

Lam DRY RESIST (Aether) is deposited and developed without wet chemistry; Lam describes it that way. Lam's claims are company statements and are not independently checked here.

Redeposited resist makes bridges: a precipitate left in the rinse can settle between two lines and join them. Slow dissolution is one cause of such defects, so develop-process changes are tested against defect counts.

# 6. Chokepoints

Chokepoints: Tokyo Electron (8035.T) and Screen (7735.T) dominate the coater/developer track market, about 88% together by one secondary estimate. Resist and developer supply and developer purity are the other pinch points.
