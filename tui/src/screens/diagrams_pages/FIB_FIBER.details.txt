== CORE
# What it does
The core is the central glass channel that carries the light: about 8.2 um across in single-mode fiber and 50 um in OM3/OM4 multimode fiber, with a slightly higher refractive index than the cladding.
# Why it matters
Its size decides single-mode or multimode, which sets the lasers you can use and how far the signal can go.
# Source
Corning ecatalog; textbook.

== CLADDING
# What it does
The cladding is 125 um of glass around the core with a slightly lower refractive index, so the boundary reflects light back into the core.
# Why it matters
Its 125 um diameter is standard across fiber types, so connectors and splicers work with any of them.
# Source
Corning SMF-28 Ultra datasheet.

== COATING
# What it does
A dual-layer acrylate coating, 242 um across (or 200 um in reduced-diameter fiber), is applied as the fiber is drawn to protect the glass.
# Why it matters
Bare glass breaks at the first scratch; thinner 200 um coatings fit more fibers into a cable, which dense AI cabling needs.
# Source
Corning SMF-28 Ultra product page.

== TOTAL INTERNAL REFLECTION
# What it does
When light inside a higher-index material meets a lower-index one at a shallow enough angle, all of it reflects; none escapes.
# Why it matters
It is why light can travel kilometres in a fiber: the core-cladding boundary is a perfect mirror for light travelling nearly along the fiber.
# Source
Textbook (Snell's law).

== SINGLE-MODE
# What it does
Single-mode fiber's narrow core carries light as one guided mode; at 1310 nm its mode field is 9.2 um wide, a little wider than the core.
# Why it matters
With only one path, pulses don't smear, so links reach kilometres; it needs InP lasers and precise alignment.
# Source
Corning SMF-28 Ultra datasheet.

== MULTIMODE
# What it does
Multimode fiber's 50 um core carries many paths at once and accepts light easily from cheap 850 nm VCSELs.
# Why it matters
The paths have different lengths, so pulses smear (modal dispersion) and reach is limited to about 100 m at today's rates.
# Source
Textbook.

== ATTENUATION
# What it does
Attenuation is the light lost per kilometre to scattering and absorption: at most 0.32 dB/km at 1310 nm and 0.18 dB/km at 1550 nm for SMF-28 Ultra.
# Why it matters
Inside a data center the glass loses under 1 dB; connectors and splices dominate the loss budget instead.
# Source
Corning SMF-28 Ultra datasheet.

== LATENCY
# What it does
Light in glass travels at about two thirds of its vacuum speed, about 5 microseconds per kilometre.
# Why it matters
It is a floor on how fast distant GPUs or data centers can talk; hollow-core fiber, guiding light in air, cuts it by about a third.
# Source
Textbook (group index about 1.47).
