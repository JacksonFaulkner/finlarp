# 3. The mechanics of each step

  slicing     brittle micro-fracture by fixed diamond grit; kerf loss = effective wire diameter + wobble
  lapping     loose abrasive slurry between two plates, both faces; sets flatness + parallelism
  grinding    fixed-abrasive wheel alternative; resin-bond wheel removes 10-20 um on one side
  etching     alkali or acid bath removes the damaged layer lapping leaves behind
  polishing   chemical + mechanical: chemistry softens the surface, abrasive removes it
  CMP law     Preston: removal rate RR = K*P + C   (K Preston constant, P pressure, C zero-pressure term)
  oxide CMP   removal rises with pH for silica slurries; pitting seen at pH 13; ceria peaks near pH 8-9
  surface     chemistry softens or oxidises the top layer, abrasive wipes it off (textbook picture)
  planarity   pad asperities + slurry flow average out high spots; pressure P and speed set the rate
  damage      sawing leaves a cracked layer microns deep; every later step exists to remove it

# 4. By the numbers

  kerf, slurry saw        about 150 um (ITRPV roadmap via TaiyangNews)
  kerf, diamond wire      about 125 um today, path to about 85 um
  fine wire               under 35 um diameter is where research is heading (Ge et al. 2026)
  cut speed               diamond wire about 2x the speed of the older slurry technology
  lapping stock removal   80 to 200 um total for double-side lapping; one 200-300 mm example 80 um, 40
      min
  double-side rough grind about 45-75 um removed before lapping (patent example)
  chemical etch           about 15-25 um removed after lapping
  double-side polish      10 to 20 um total; front-side polish 10-15 um, 5-15 min
  300 mm patent example   acid etch 10 um, gas-phase etch 2 um, final polish 5 um on the front
  end result              775 um +/- 25 um thick, TTV 5 um or less (vendor datasheet, see WAFER)

  (kerf matters: at 125 um kerf and a ~775 um wafer, about one sixth of the ingot length turns to swarf,
   my arithmetic from the numbers above; note PV roadmap kerf numbers are for solar-grade wafers)

# 7. Chokepoints and risks

  - wire breakage and wear: wear raises wire deflection, thickness variation and kerf (Springer 2026)
  - thinner wire means less kerf but weaker wire; the trade-off limits how fast kerf can shrink
  - CMP slurries and pads are consumables bought forever: a few vendors, qualified per fab line
  - two-supplier CMP tool market; Chinese domestic toolmakers are pushing in (AMAT FY2025 10-K)
  - ceria and silica abrasives: raw-material and purity supply sits upstream of this step

  Process control points (textbook, no number sourced):
    wire tension and speed, ingot feed rate, coolant temperature (thermal bow of the wafer)
    plate pressure and slurry mix in lapping; etch bath temperature and time; pad wear in CMP

# 8. Why it matters downstream

  A polished wafer is the flat reference for lithography: later CMP in the fab reuses the same physics
  on deposited films (STI, copper, tungsten). So the Preston law above runs on both ends of the chip.

# 9. Caveats

  Removal amounts are patent examples, not a standard; each wafer maker tunes its own sequence.
  Market-share items are from market-research summaries and old analyst data; verify before use.
  Diamond-wire kerf numbers are solar-industry roadmap values, shown as the best sourced kerf data.
