== CO-PACKAGED OPTICS
# What it does
Co-packaged optics place optical engines on the switch chip's package, so electrical signals travel millimetres instead of across the board to front-panel modules.
# Why it matters
It removes most of the DSPs and their power; NVIDIA says its photonics switches use 3.5x less power than with pluggable transceivers.
# Source
optics.org, March 2025.

== OPTICAL ENGINE
# What it does
An optical engine is a silicon-photonics chip (modulators, waveguides, photodiodes) with an electronic driver and TIA chip stacked on top, sitting beside the switch chip.
# Why it matters
It does a pluggable module's job in a fraction of the space and power, but is soldered in, not swappable.
# Source
wccftech; TSMC COUPE coverage.

== MICRO-RING
# What it does
A micro-ring modulator is a ring waveguide a few microns across that couples light at one resonant wavelength; a voltage shifts the resonance to modulate the light.
# Why it matters
Rings are tiny and need little drive power, which suits dense CPO engines, but drift with temperature and need active tuning; NVIDIA's run at 200 Gb/s.
# Source
wccftech (NVIDIA 200G MRM).

== EXTERNAL LASER
# What it does
An external laser source is a pluggable module at the front panel holding CW lasers that feed light to the optical engines through fiber.
# Why it matters
It keeps the most failure-prone part swappable and cool; reports from Hot Chips 2025 say a 1.6 Tb/s link needs two lasers instead of eight.
# Source
NADDOD summary of NVIDIA Hot Chips 2025.

== FRONT PANEL
# What it does
The front panel is the faceplate of a switch where pluggable modules sit in cages, connected to the switch chip by board traces.
# Why it matters
At 200G per lane those traces lose so much signal each module needs a DSP; with CPO the panel holds only fiber and external lasers.
# Source
Textbook; optics.org.

== POWER PER PORT
# What it does
Power per port is the watts used to move one port's data on and off the switch, counting the module, its DSP and the host SerDes.
# Why it matters
One report puts a 1.6T pluggable port at about 25 W and a CPO port at about 9 W; across a 512-port switch that is kilowatts.
# Source
TokenRing report, January 2026; NVIDIA via optics.org.

== SERVICEABILITY
# What it does
Serviceability is how easily a failed part can be replaced: a pluggable module is pulled and swapped in seconds, an optical engine is soldered to the switch package.
# Why it matters
It is the main objection to CPO; keeping lasers external and improving engine reliability are the answers so far.
# Source
Textbook; NVIDIA claims of 13x laser reliability (secondary coverage).

== COUPE
# What it does
COUPE (Compact Universal Photonic Engine) is TSMC's process that stacks an electronic chip directly on a photonic chip with short vertical connections.
# Why it matters
It is how NVIDIA's CPO engines are made, tying co-packaged optics to TSMC's advanced packaging capacity.
# Source
wccftech, NVIDIA Spectrum-X Photonics.
