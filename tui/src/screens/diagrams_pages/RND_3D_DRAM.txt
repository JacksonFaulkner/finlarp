# 1. What it is: DRAM cells built upward

3D DRAM builds the memory cell upward instead of flat, so each bit takes less floor space. It comes in two steps. First, the transistor stands up: a vertical channel transistor (VCT) gives a 4F2 cell. Samsung showed a 16 Gb chip built this way at ISSCC 2026 [1]. Second, whole layers of cells are stacked, the way 3D NAND stacks flash. Samsung's slides put that in the early 2030s [3].

# 2. The parts

1T1C CELL       one transistor and one capacitor: the capacitor holds the bit as charge
6F2 / 4F2       cell area in units of F, the smallest feature: 2F x 3F today, 2F x 2F next
VCT             vertical channel transistor: source, channel and drain stacked in a pillar
VG              vertical gate: SK hynix's name for its vertical transistor
CAPACITOR       in a VCT cell it sits directly on top of the transistor
PERIPHERY       the sense amps, decoders and I/O that run the cell array
COP / PUC       cell on periphery: the periphery wafer bonded under the array wafer
HYBRID BOND     copper pads and oxide joined face to face, wafer to wafer
VS-DRAM         Samsung's stacked DRAM: layers of sideways cells on a vertical bit line
FLOATING BODY   a transistor body with no tie to the substrate, so charge piles up in it
IGZO            indium gallium zinc oxide: an oxide channel that leaks very little

# 3. From planar to stacked, in order

1. today's cell lies flat: transistor in the silicon, tall capacitor on top, 6F2
2. shrinking stalls: a narrower capacitor has to grow taller to hold the same charge
3. the transistor stands up as a pillar, capacitor on top: a 4F2 cell
4. the periphery moves to its own wafer and is bonded under the array
5. the cells are laid on their sides and stacked in layers sharing a vertical bit line

# 4. Why DRAM has to go vertical

A DRAM cell stores its bit in a tall, narrow capacitor. When the cell shrinks, the capacitor's radius shrinks too, so it must grow taller to keep its charge, and tall capacitors are hard to make with good yield [4]. Today's cells take about 6F2 (2F x 3F). A 4F2 cell (2F x 2F) is a third smaller, which fits about 50% more cells at the same F [5]. The Elec estimates 30-50% more density per die [2]. Samsung's 10a node, the first below the 10 nm class, is estimated at 9.5-9.7 nm [2].

# 5. The 4F2 cell: a vertical channel transistor

In a VCT the source, channel and drain sit in one upright pillar, so the transistor takes no more floor space than the capacitor above it [2][5]. The word line wraps the pillar as the gate; the bit line connects at the source [6]. Samsung is reported to be moving the channel from silicon to IGZO to cut leakage, and to be weighing molybdenum to replace titanium nitride in the word line [2].

# 6. Wafer-to-wafer bonding: periphery under the array

Samsung's ISSCC 2026 paper is a 16 Gb, 10 nm-class test chip with 4F2 VCT cells built on one wafer and the periphery on another, joined by wafer-to-wafer hybrid copper bonding (cell-on-peripheral). Samsung says this gives over 20% more dies per wafer under the same design rules; the chip reads and writes [1]. The periphery wafer is made with a logic process [2]. SK hynix plans the same split, with circuits bonded below the cell area [7].

# 7. Stacked 3D DRAM: layers of sideways cells

Stacked 3D DRAM turns the 1T1C cell on its side: horizontal capacitors, gate-all-around transistors, horizontal word lines, and vertical bit lines running down through the layers. Samsung's VLSI 2026 paper shows 16 layers this way, with the periphery on a separate bonded wafer [8]. SK hynix showed 5 layers at VLSI 2024 with 56.1% yield and said 32-192 layers are needed for wide use [9]. A Micron engineer estimated in 2021 that 200-300 layers are needed to beat planar DRAM on cost [4].

# 8. The weak spot: the floating body

In a vertical pillar the transistor body is cut off from the silicon below, so charge can build up in it and change how the transistor switches [5]. One fix is a back gate: SK hynix's VLSI 2026 4F2 VG cell adds a shared back-gate transistor to control the threshold voltage [8]. Another is an oxide channel: Kioxia and Nanya's junctionless IGZO vertical transistor is reported immune to the floating-body effect, with leakage below 1 aA and retention over 100 seconds (IEDM 2024) [10].

# 9. Who is building it, timing and risks

Samsung (005930.KS) reportedly made working 10a dies in March 2026; the reported plan is development in 2026, qualification in 2027 and mass production in 2028, with 3D DRAM from 10d [2]. Samsung has not committed to it [5]. SK hynix (000660.KS) reportedly plans 4F2 at 10b [2]. Micron (MU) skips 4F2: "It's the capacitor pitch that defines the DRAM" [5]. CXMT plans 4F2 DDR5 RDIMMs by end of 2026, possibly only partly 4F2 [11]. NEO Semiconductor claims IGZO 3D X-DRAM up to 128 layers [12].

- The dates are company targets or press reports; no 4F2 or stacked DRAM is on sale.
- Extra steps and new materials can eat the density gain, so smaller cells may not mean cheaper bits [5].
- Stacked DRAM needs dozens to hundreds of layers before it pays [4][9].
