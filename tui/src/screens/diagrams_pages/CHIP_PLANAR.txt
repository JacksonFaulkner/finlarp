# 1. What happened: the planar era, from free scaling to the leakage wall

A planar transistor is a flat channel under a flat gate. For decades shrinking it was free: DENNARD SCALING cut every dimension and the voltage together, so speed rose while power density stayed flat. Around 2005 the threshold voltage could no longer fall without leakage exploding, and supply voltage stalled near 1 V: the LEAKAGE WALL. STRAINED SI, then HIGH-K METAL GATE, then FD-SOI patched planar from 90 nm to 22/28 nm before the industry moved to fins. Planar still makes most wafers at MATURE NODES, 28 nm and above.

# 2. The planar cross-section and where it leaks

An NMOS: n+ source and drain either side of a gate on a thin dielectric (SiON, later HfO2), all on p-type bulk silicon shared by every transistor on the die. Electrons flow source to drain in the inversion layer. Four leakage paths: subthreshold (drain to source below Vt), gate tunnelling through the oxide, junction/GIDL leakage, and punch-through in the body when the gate is short. The gate sees only the top of the channel; the drain's field reaches the source through the body below.

# 3. Short channels lose control

SHORT CHANNEL: as the gate length shrinks, the drain's field meets the source barrier. DIBL: the drain lowers that barrier, so Vt falls as drain voltage rises (Vt roll-off). Swing S = (kT/q) ln10 (1 + Cdep/Cox), floor about 60 mV/decade at 300 K; each 60 mV of Vt lost is 10x more off-state leakage. WHY PLANAR ENDED: the gate cannot cut off the path below the surface. NODE NAME: the "N nm" label no longer equals any real gate length.

# 4. Strain and high-k metal gate

STRAINED SI: SiGe in the PMOS source/drain compresses the channel and a nitride cap stretches the NMOS (Intel 90 nm: PMOS +25%). HIGH-K METAL GATE: HfO2 lets the oxide be physically thicker, cutting gate tunnelling, and a metal gate sets Vt; gate-last arrived at 45-28 nm.

# 5. Who still ships planar silicon

MATURE NODES (28 nm and above) were 36% of pure-play foundry revenue in 2025, down from 54% in 2021 (Counterpoint). UMC (2303.TW, UMC): 22/28 nm was 35-40% of revenue through 2025; 2025 revenue NT$237.5B, +2.3%; 40 nm and below were 53% of Q4 wafer revenue. GLOBALFOUNDRIES (GFS): FDX, RF and automotive, FY25 revenue $6.79B. SMIC (0981.HK): mostly 28 nm and above, mature and domestic.

# 6. Dennard scaling arithmetic

Scale all dimensions and the voltage by 1/k (k about 1.4 per node): gate delay x 1/k, capacitance x 1/k, voltage x 1/k, power per gate x 1/k^2, gates per area x k^2, so power density x 1. It ended in the mid-2000s when Vt and oxide stopped scaling: Ioff grows exponentially as Vt falls, so Vdd stalled near 1 V, and more transistors at the same power meant dark silicon and multicore.

# 7. FD-SOI: a thin film on buried oxide

FD-SOI builds the transistor in an ultra-thin, undoped silicon film (a few nm) on a buried oxide (BOX). The body is too thin to hold depletion charge, so there is no random-dopant variation, junction capacitance is low, and the BOX blocks the leakage path under the channel. SOITEC makes the wafers with Smart Cut. BODY BIAS applied under the BOX tunes Vt after fabrication, trading speed against leakage. GlobalFoundries ships it as 22FDX.

# 8. Who makes it

TSMC (TSM, 28 nm still a large line), Intel (INTC, which shipped the first 22 nm FinFET in 2011), UMC, GlobalFoundries (GFS), Soitec (SOI.PA) and SMIC (0981.HK).

# 9. Chokepoints and risks

- Mature-node overcapacity from China adds price pressure (UMC wafer shipments +12.3% in 2025, revenue +2.3%).
- FD-SOI is a niche (IoT, RF, automotive); the mass market moved to FinFET at 16/14 nm and below.
- Notes: scaling steps are textbook; strain figures are Intel 2004-era disclosures via secondary summaries; gate lengths of "N nm" nodes vary by vendor.
