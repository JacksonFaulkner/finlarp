# 3. Particle removal mechanism

 ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
 │   SC1: NH4OH etches Si a little, H2O2 regrows oxide; each cycle undercuts the particle  │
 │   at high pH both surfaces share a charge sign, so the particle is repelled             │
 │     o  particle                    ◀── pH about 10 (textbook)                           │
 │   ───┴─────────── wafer             ◀── megasonic bubbles shear the boundary layer      │
 │   too much power: bubble collapse (cavitation) damaged 55 nm lines in a patent test [3] │
 └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

# 4. Recipes and chemistry

  SC1   H2O : H2O2 : NH4OH   1000:1:1 to 1:1:1 by volume; preferred ~100:1:1 to 5:1:1 [2]
        30-80 C, 5-30 min (patent); vendor bench 40:1:1 at about 50 C for 20 min [2][4]
  SC2   H2O : H2O2 : HCl     vendor 160:4:1 at about 50 C for 20 min; metals become soluble chlorides [4]
  SPM   H2SO4 : H2O2         patent example 99% : 1% at 140 C for 10 min, then SC1 at 55 C for 10 min [2]
  DHF   HF : H2O             usually 1000:1 or 200:1; removes native or thin oxide [2]
  order HF, then SC1, then SC2, DI water rinse between each [2]
  Si + 2H2O2 ──▶ SiO2 + 2H2O    then    SiO2 + 6HF ──▶ H2SiF6 + 2H2O      (textbook, unsourced)

# 5. By the numbers

  megasonic         patents predict damage-free at <= 1 MHz; 0.1 ms pulse cycles gave
                    no damage, 2 ms gave 1,216 damage sites on 55 nm features [3]
  ultrasonic bath   80 kHz and 120 kHz selectable on one university RCA tank [4]
  DI water          18.2 megohm-cm resistivity is the textbook spec (no source found this run)
  vendors           SCREEN, Tokyo Electron, Lam and SEMES lead single-wafer clean per aggregators [5]

# 8. Clean inside WFE, and who sells it

  Etch + clean was ~27% of WFE in early 2022 and ~19% in H1 2024 (Yole; dry and wet combined) [6]
  SCREEN (7735.T)      claims #1 in single-wafer, batch and scrubbers; Tokyo Electron (8035.T) also big
  Lam Research (LRCX)  sells clean beside etch and deposition; SEMES is a Samsung affiliate (textbook)
  Wet ETCH (not clean) shares on one 2025 page: Applied 15.7%, TEL 13.8%, Lam 12.7%, Hitachi H-T 7.2% [7]
  Chokepoints: ultra-pure chemicals and DI water supply, HF and H2SO4 waste handling, and gentle clean
  for GAA and 3D NAND where fragile structures and deep holes limit megasonic power (textbook).

# 10. Drying and the pattern-collapse problem

  After the last rinse the water must leave without leaving watermarks or pulling lines together.
 ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
 │   water between fins              water evaporates                after IPA / Marangoni dry      │
 │    |▒|  ≈≈≈  |▒|  ≈≈  |▒|          |▒|   ≈   |▒|    |▒|           |▒|       |▒|      |▒|         │
 │    |▒|  ≈≈≈  |▒|  ≈≈  |▒|          |▒|  ╲ ╱  |▒|    |▒|           |▒|       |▒|      |▒|         │
 │    |▒|__≈≈≈__|▒|__≈≈__|▒|          |▒|__╲╱___|▒|____|▒|           |▒|_______|▒|______|▒|         │
 │   surface tension of the meniscus pulls tall, thin lines together (capillary collapse, textbook) │
 └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
  Fixes (textbook): IPA vapour to lower surface tension, spin dry with N2, or supercritical CO2.

# 11. What the clean has to remove (textbook, unsourced numbers omitted)

  particles    SC1 + megasonic             organics    SPM (hot sulfuric/peroxide), ozone, plasma ash
  metal ions   SC2 or dilute acids         native oxide DHF; leaves a hydrogen-terminated Si surface
  residues     after etch: solvent strips  roughness   keep SC1 gentle, it etches Si slowly

  Order inside a recipe (textbook): hot SPM first for organics, then DI rinse, DHF to strip the chemical
  oxide SPM leaves, SC1 for particles, SC2 for metals, final rinse and dry.
  Recipes vary by fab and layer; the ratios in section 4 are patent and vendor examples, not a standard.
