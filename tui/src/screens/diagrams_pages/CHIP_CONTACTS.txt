# 1. What happens: wire the transistor up to the first metal

Contacts are the vertical plugs from the silicon up to the first metal layer, the "middle of line" between the transistor and the wiring. PRE-CLEAN: etch the native oxide off. SILICIDE: Ni or Ti on the source/drain, annealed into NiSi. LINER/BARRIER: a few nm of Ti/TiN by ALD or CVD. W FILL: tungsten from WF6 and H2. CMP: polish flat, stopping on the ILD. VIA0 / M1: the first via and metal land on the plug.

# 2. From M1 down to silicon

M1 wires at a 30-40 nm pitch; via0 lands on a plug; tungsten plugs with liners stand over the source, drain and gate in SiO2 or low-k ILD. A SiN cap sits on the metal gate (self-aligned). Silicide (NiSi or TiSi, a few nm) sits between each plug and the doped epi (above 1e20 /cm3); its barrier height to silicon sets the contact resistance. A gate contact landing over the active channel is COAG.

# 3. Contact types and the tricks that let them shrink

TRENCH CONTACT (TS): a long bar strapping the source/drain epi. GATE CONTACT: a plug on the gate metal, which used to sit off the active area. COAG: contact over active gate. SAC CAP: a SiN cap on the gate that tolerates misalignment. SELECTIVE W: tungsten grown bottom-up, with no seam and less barrier. Co / Ru / Mo: tungsten alternatives with better thin-film resistivity. Older nodes etched one hole per plug; now trench contacts use self-aligned etch stops.

# 4. Why contact resistance became the bottleneck

Total resistance is channel plus source/drain epi plus contact plus local wire. Plugs are about 15-20 nm wide, but tungsten's electron mean free path is 19.1 nm, so surfaces scatter. Liner and TiN take a few nm per side of a 15 nm hole: much of the width isn't conductor. The Schottky barrier to n-Si is 0.65-0.7 eV for NiSi, 0.58 eV for TiSi2. Contact area falls with pitch squared, so rho_c / A climbs fast. CONTACT RESISTANCE: R = rho_c / A plus plug body plus liner. LINER SQUEEZE: TiN doesn't shrink, so plug body resistance rises. BARRIER TUNING: dopant segregation cuts the barrier from 0.7 to 0.34 eV.

# 5. By the numbers

bulk resistivity        W ~5.6, Co ~6.2, Ru ~7.1, Mo ~5.3 uOhm-cm; Cu ~1.7 (approximate)
contact rho_c           pFET ~3e-9, nFET ~1e-8 ohm-cm2 (older patent background)
Schottky barrier        NiSi 0.65-0.7 eV; TiSi2 0.58 eV; N2+ pre-silicide implant 0.7 -> 0.34 eV
Applied Volta W         "up to 90%" lower contact resistance, CD dependent (vendor claim)
Applied selective W     over 40% contact-resistance drop vs conventional W (vendor)

Intel 10 nm COAG        about 10% standard-cell height reduction (Intel claim, secondary)
TSMC N2 (IEDM 2024)     better gate-contact R, up to 20% R+C cut in middle of line (reported)

# 6. Applied Materials, the contact-metal specialist

Applied Materials' FY2025 revenue was $28.37B: Semiconductor Systems 73%, Global Services 23%, corporate/other 4%. Semiconductor Systems ($20.80B) was 67% foundry/logic/other, 26% DRAM, 7% NAND.

# 7. New metals: cobalt, ruthenium, molybdenum

Intel moved local interconnect to cobalt at 10 nm (2017), and cobalt and molybdenum are replacing tungsten where thin-film resistivity matters more than bulk. Barrierless and selective fills save the liner's width but raise adhesion and diffusion risk.

# 8. Who makes it

Applied Materials (AMAT): Endura PVD/CVD/ALD, cobalt and selective tungsten. Lam Research (LRCX): ALTUS CVD+ALD tungsten and molybdenum for contacts, vias and wordlines (FY2025 $18.44B). TSMC (TSM) and Intel (INTC) design the contact scheme; Intel was first with cobalt and COAG at 10 nm. Samsung, SK hynix and Micron buy the tools; Tokyo Electron (8035.T) sells CVD/ALD. Synopsys and Cadence extract contact RC.

# 9. Chokepoints and risks

- New metals (Mo, Ru) need new tools and qualification.
- Barrierless fill raises adhesion and diffusion risk.
- Contact RC erodes node-to-node gains.
