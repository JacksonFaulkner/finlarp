# 1. What happens: stack the sheets, carve them, release them, wrap the gate

A gate-all-around (GAA) transistor splits the fin into a stack of thin silicon sheets and wraps the gate around all four sides of each. It is built in steps: grow alternating Si and SiGe layers (SUPERLATTICE), etch the stack into fins (FIN PATTERN), put a placeholder gate on (DUMMY GATE), recess the SiGe and fill with nitride (INNER SPACER), grow the source and drain (S/D EPI), etch the SiGe away to free the sheets (RELEASE), then fill every gap with high-k and metal (HK/METAL GATE).

# 2. Along the channel

Current flows from the source epi (SiP for n, SiGeB for p) through each silicon sheet to the drain epi. Between the sheets, high-k and work-function metal wrap all four sides. Inner spacers (SiN or SiOCN) isolate the gate from the source and drain at every level. The SiGe layers were sacrificial: they set the gaps, are etched away, and the gate metal takes their place. A punch-through stop under the stack blocks the parasitic bottom channel.

# 3. Before and after release

Before release: Si sheet, SiGe, Si, SiGe, Si, SiGe. After the inner spacer: the SiGe is recessed at its ends and filled with nitride. After release: the SiGe is gone and gate metal fills the gaps. The key step is etching SiGe sideways with high selectivity to silicon; any silicon lost thins the sheet and shifts the threshold.

# 4. New process modules

SUPERLATTICE: 3-4 Si/SiGe pairs with about 1 nm thickness control. INNER SPACER: an isotropic SiGe recess, then fill and etch back. RELEASE: lateral SiGe removal needing Si:SiGe selectivity. HK/METAL GATE: ALD into gaps of about 10 nm; the whole stack must fit. S/D EPI: grown from the sheet edges, for strain and low contact resistance. Each module is a source of variation.

# 5. Sheet width: a knob FinFET did not have

SHEET WIDTH is drawn per device on the same stack height: wide sheets for drive and speed, narrow sheets for low leakage and small cells. Effective width per stack is about N sheets x (W + 2T), T the sheet thickness. TSMC says N2 allows cells with different nanosheet widths on the same chip. A FINFET could only add whole fins; a NANOSHEET can be any width.

# 6. Backside power

Conventional chips route power and signals through the same front-side metal, so power paths are long and thin. Backside power moves the power rails under a thinned substrate, reached through nano-TSVs: Intel's PowerVia debuts on 18A; TSMC's Super Power Rail on A16 contacts the source/drain directly. Signals get the front metal to themselves.

# 7. Node comparison (vendor claims, not equal conditions)

Samsung 3GAA (SF3E), 2022, vs 5 nm: -45% power, +23% performance, -16% area. TSMC N2, 4Q25, vs N3E: +10-15% speed or -25-30% power, about 1.15x mixed density. Intel 18A, 2025, vs Intel 3: about 25% frequency or -36% power at 1.1 V, over 30% density. TSMC A16, 2026+, vs N2P: +8-10% speed, -15-20% power, up to 8-10% density.

# 8. Roadmap and who makes it

FINFET to ~3 nm class; NANOSHEET at N2, 18A, SF2; FORKSHEET puts a wall between n and p sheets (imec: extends nanosheets to A10); CFET stacks NMOS over PMOS (imec: monolithic CFET at A7, after 2030). NODES: Samsung SF3, TSMC N2, Intel 18A, TSMC A16. TOOLS: Applied Materials (AMAT), Lam Research (LRCX), Tokyo Electron and ASML; Applied puts GAA transistor-module spending at about $7B per 100K wafer starts a month against about $6B for FinFET, and its GAA revenue at about $2.5B in FY2024, guided to roughly double. Lam's Akara etch (2025) targets GAA.

# 9. Chokepoints and risks: yield and cost

YIELD/COST (secondary reports, not audited): Samsung SF3E 50-60% (2024), SF3 second generation about 20%, SF2 test about 30% (Feb 2025), against TSMC N3E FinFET about 82-86%. TSMC N2 wafers reported above $30,000 against about $18,000 for N3 (unconfirmed). Numbers are vendor claims and press reports under different conditions; sheet counts and SiGe percentages are textbook.
