# 1. What happens: chip on wafer, then on substrate

CoWoS assembly happens in two stages. CHIP ON WAFER: known-good logic and HBM dies are bonded face down onto a whole 300 mm interposer wafer (thermocompression or mass reflow of ~40 um microbumps), underfilled and molded, and the wafer is thinned from ~775 um to ~100 um to expose its TSVs, which then get C4 bumps. ON SUBSTRATE: the wafer is cut into interposers, each is bonded to an organic substrate by C4 REFLOW, underfilled again (UNDERFILL FLOW), lidded and tested (TEST). Every step multiplies yield (YIELD STACKUP), and CAPACITY at this stage has limited AI GPU output.

# 2. The parts

CHIP ON WAFER     dies bonded onto an uncut interposer wafer
ON SUBSTRATE      the cut interposer bonded onto the package substrate
REFLOW            heating that melts solder into joints
UNDERFILL FLOW    epoxy wicked under each level and cured
TEST              electrical test before and after assembly
YIELD STACKUP     package yield is the product of every die's and step's yield
CAPACITY          CoWoS output, measured in wafers per month
OSAT              outsourced assembly and test houses, such as ASE and Amkor

# 3. One package, in order

1. dies placed face down on the interposer wafer, bonded (logic first, then HBM)
2. underfill and mold, then the wafer back-ground to reveal TSVs and C4 bumps formed
3. the wafer diced along its scribe streets into interposers
4. each bonded to an organic (ABF) substrate by C4 reflow (~250 C peak) and underfilled again
5. TIM and lid, final test and burn-in, ship

# 4. Yield stacks up

A package with one logic die, eight HBM stacks and an interposer is only good if all of them and every joint are good, so YIELD STACKUP multiplies: at 99% per part, ten parts give about 90%. That is why only known-good dies are assembled.

# 5. Capacity: the bottleneck

Almost every high-end AI accelerator uses CoWoS, so monthly CoWoS CAPACITY strongly limits how many ship. A 300 mm wafer holds only about 15-20 of the largest interposers (illustrative), so bigger packages use more capacity each. In August 2026 Cerebras still described CoWoS packaging and HBM as supply limited (8-K). NVIDIA reportedly took 40-50% of capacity in 2024; TSMC planned to double capacity in 2023 and, on its Q1 2026 call, said advanced packaging remained strained (secondary reports). Blackwell-class parts use more capacity per package because their interposers are bigger.

# 6. Outsourced assembly

OSAT houses add capacity: ASE (ASX) and Amkor (AMKR) take substrate attach and test, and UMC supplies interposers (TrendForce).

# 7. Test

TEST happens at wafer sort (probe cards on every logic die, so only known-good dies are assembled), after chip-on-wafer (probing the interposer site to check die-to-HBM links), and on the finished package in a socket at full power, with burn-in to weed out early failures. A defect found late scraps the whole assembly with all its HBM.

# 8. Who makes it

TSMC (TSM) does most CoWoS; ASE (ASX) and Amkor (AMKR) as OSATs; UMC for interposers. NVIDIA (NVDA) and AMD buy the capacity.

# 9. Chokepoints and risks

- Packaging capacity, not wafers, has limited accelerator shipments.
- One supplier does most CoWoS.
- Late defects scrap expensive parts.
- Warpage: silicon and organic substrate expand differently, so large packages bow at reflow and stress the edge joints.
- Notes: capacity and share figures are press reports and estimates.
