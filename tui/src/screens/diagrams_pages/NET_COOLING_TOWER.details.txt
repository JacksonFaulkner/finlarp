== COOLING TOWER
# What it does
A cooling tower cools water by letting a small fraction evaporate into air drawn through it.
# Why it matters
It is the cheapest way to reject a water-cooled plant's heat, at the cost of water.
# Source
SPX Cooling Tower Fundamentals.

== BASIN
# What it does
The basin collects cooled water at the bottom of the tower for the pumps.
# Why it matters
It is where make-up enters, blowdown leaves and water treatment is dosed.
# Source
Textbook.

== LOUVERS
# What it does
Louvers are slanted slats on the air inlets that let air in and keep water from splashing out.
# Why it matters
They also block sunlight, limiting algae.
# Source
Textbook.

== FILL
# What it does
Fill is packs of corrugated PVC sheets that spread falling water into thin films.
# Why it matters
More wet surface means a closer approach to wet-bulb for the same tower size.
# Source
SPX Cooling Tower Fundamentals.

== SPRAY NOZZLES
# What it does
Nozzles on a header distribute the warm water evenly over the fill.
# Why it matters
Dry patches in the fill waste capacity.
# Source
Textbook.

== DRIFT ELIMINATORS
# What it does
Chevron blades force the leaving air to turn; droplets cannot follow and drain back.
# Why it matters
They cut drift to ~0.001% of flow or less, saving water and limiting aerosols.
# Source
CTI; SPX.

== FAN
# What it does
An induced-draft fan on top pulls air up through the fill and out of the tower.
# Why it matters
Variable-speed drives slow it when it is cool, saving power.
# Source
Textbook.

== MAKE-UP WATER
# What it does
Make-up water replaces what evaporates and what is drained as blowdown, through a meter.
# Why it matters
It is the tower's water bill: ~1.5-2 L per kWh of heat rejected.
# Source
Textbook.

== BLOWDOWN
# What it does
Blowdown bleeds off basin water so minerals left by evaporation do not build up.
# Why it matters
Too little causes scale and corrosion; too much wastes water.
# Source
Textbook.

== HEAT OUT
# What it does
Heat out is the last step of data-center cooling: the heat leaves to the outdoor air as vapour (wet towers) or warm air (dry coolers).
# Why it matters
Every watt the campus draws ends here; how it leaves decides water use.
# Source
Textbook.
