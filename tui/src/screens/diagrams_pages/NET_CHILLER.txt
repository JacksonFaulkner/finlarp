# 1. What happens: water out, heat lifted, heat dumped

The CHILLER sends CHILLED WATER to the hall's air handlers or liquid-cooling units and takes warm water back. Inside, a REFRIGERANT loop lifts that heat: it boils in the EVAPORATOR, is squeezed hot by the COMPRESSOR, gives its heat to outdoor air in the CONDENSER and drops in pressure through the EXPANSION VALVE.

# 2. The parts

CHILLER           a refrigeration machine that cools a water loop
CHILLED WATER     the building loop, traditionally ~7 C, now often 15-20 C or warmer
EVAPORATOR        shell-and-tube exchanger where refrigerant boils and takes the water's heat
COMPRESSOR        screw or centrifugal machine that squeezes the vapour hot
CONDENSER         coil (air-cooled) or shell (water-cooled) that sheds the heat
EXPANSION VALVE   drops the pressure so the liquid flashes cold
REFRIGERANT       the working fluid: R-134a, moving to R-1234ze, R-513A, R-1233zd
FREE COOLING      cooling with cold outdoor air while the compressor rests
DRY COOLER        coil and fans with no compressor at all

# 3. The loop, in order

1. warm water makes the refrigerant boil in the EVAPORATOR
2. the vapour is drawn into the COMPRESSOR
3. it leaves hot and dense, around 60-80 C
4. in the CONDENSER it gives up its heat and turns liquid
5. the EXPANSION VALVE drops its pressure; part flashes to vapour, the mix turns cold
6. the cold mix returns to the evaporator

# 4. How much heat

Chillers are rated in tons of refrigeration: 1 ton = 3.517 kW, so 1 MW of cooling is about 285 tons. Large air-cooled units run roughly 1-2 MW; big water-cooled centrifugals go higher. A 130 kW GB200 NVL72 rack needs about 37 tons, so a large campus served by chillers needs dozens of units.

# 5. Compressors

Screw compressors (two meshing rotors) dominate mid-size air-cooled chillers. Centrifugal compressors spin an impeller and dominate large water-cooled plants. Magnetic-bearing, oil-free designs (Danfoss Turbocor, York YZ and others) float the shaft, cutting friction; the best water-cooled plants run near 0.5 kW per ton.

# 6. COP and water temperature

COP is heat moved divided by electrical power. Air-cooled chillers manage about 3; water-cooled about 6-7. The compressor's work depends on the lift from chilled-water to outdoor temperature, so each degree warmer on the chilled water saves roughly 2-3% of compressor power. Liquid-cooled GPUs accept 30 C+ facility water, which shrinks or eliminates the lift.

# 7. Free cooling and dry coolers

A FREE COOLING chiller adds a coil that pre-cools the return water with outdoor air, so the compressor can stop when it is cold enough. A DRY COOLER drops the compressor entirely and suits 30-45 C liquid loops. Adiabatic pads wet the incoming air on the hottest days, using water only a few hundred hours a year.

# 8. Who makes it

Trane Technologies (TT), Carrier (CARR) and Johnson Controls' York (JCI) lead large chillers; Daikin, Mitsubishi and LG compete globally. Modine (MOD) sells data-center chillers and dry coolers (Airedale); Vertiv (VRT) sells chillers alongside CDUs and air handlers. Danfoss (private) makes Turbocor oil-free compressors. Market shares are not sourced here.

# 9. Chokepoints and risks

- HFC phase-down: EPA rules cap the GWP of refrigerants in new chillers (700 for many chiller types), forcing redesigns.
- Air-cooled chillers lose capacity in heat waves, exactly at peak load.
- Long lead times on large units while data-center demand surges.
