# 1. What happens: one module holds both ends of the conversion

A pluggable transceiver slides into a cage on the switch or server's front panel. Its edge connector takes eight electrical lanes from the host chip. Inside: a DSP, laser drivers and lasers on one side, photodiodes and TIAs on the other, lenses, and an MPO connector where the fiber plugs in. The shell is an OSFP (or QSFP-DD) with fins to shed the heat. The same module receives as well as sends.

# 2. The parts

OSFP      octal small form-factor pluggable: 8 lanes, room for about 25 W, finned shell
QSFP-DD   double-density QSFP: 8 lanes in a smaller, cooler shell
DR8       eight single-mode fibers each way, one 100G or 200G lane each, about 500 m
2xFR4     four wavelengths on one fiber (CWDM), two sets, to 2 km
SR8       eight multimode fibers, 850 nm VCSELs, short reach
DAC       direct-attach copper: no optics, a few metres, cheapest
AOC       active optical cable: transceivers built onto both ends of a fiber
MPO       multi-fiber connector: 12 or 16 fibers in one plug

# 3. One module's job, in order

1. the host's eight SerDes lanes arrive at the edge connector
2. the DSP retimes them
3. drivers and lasers turn each lane into light
4. lenses couple the light into fibers in the MPO connector
5. in the other direction, photodiodes and TIAs turn light back into lanes
6. the module's controller reports power, temperature and light levels to the host

# 4. Reach decides the type

On a board, copper traces. In the rack, a few metres: DAC copper, cheap but heavy and limited in reach. Across a row, up to about 100 m: AOC or SR8 multimode. Across a building, up to 500 m and 2 km: DR8 and 2xFR4 single-mode. Between buildings and cities: coherent modules (ZR) at 80 km and beyond.

# 5. Lanes and speeds

800G modules carry eight 100G PAM4 lanes; 1.6T modules carry eight 200G lanes using 3 nm DSPs. A DR8 module needs a separate fiber per lane, 16 in all, through an MPO connector; 2xFR4 multiplexes four wavelengths onto each fiber to use only four.

# 6. Power and heat

Listings put 1.6T DSP modules at up to about 25-26 W each. A big switch has 64 such ports, so the optics can draw over a kilowatt; the OSFP shell's fins and the switch's fans carry it away. Linear (LPO) modules cut that roughly in half or more.

# 7. Scale: modules per GPU

Large AI clusters connect every GPU through several tiers of switches, and every switch-to-switch link has a module at each end, so clusters use several modules per GPU. Volume, not just speed, is what drives the market.

# 8. Who makes it

LightCounting's 2024 ranking had Innolight first (revenue up 114% to over $3.3 billion) and Eoptolink third ($1.2 billion, up 175%), with Coherent (COHR) a long-time leader; seven of the top ten were Chinese. Lumentum (LITE) and Broadcom supply lasers; Fabrinet (FN) assembles modules under contract.

# 9. Chokepoints and risks

- Lasers (EMLs) and DSPs set how many modules can ship.
- Most volume is made in China and Southeast Asia, exposed to trade policy.
- Module power at cluster scale pushes toward LPO and co-packaged optics.
