# 4. The chemistry and physics

  radicals   CF4 + e- ──▶ CF3 + F + e-             fluorine radicals made from fluorocarbon gas
  SiO2       SiO2 + 4F ──▶ SiF4(gas) + O2          slow without ion bombardment to break Si-O bonds
  Si         Si + 4F ──▶ SiF4  or  Si + 2Cl2 ──▶ SiCl4     (textbook: Cl2 / HBr for poly-Si and fins)
  3D NAND    HF gas etches the oxide-nitride stack at cold wafer temp; PF3 catalyst raises rate [2]
  ALE        cycle = self-limiting surface modification, then low-eV Ar+ removes only that layer [4]
             Cl2 + 50 eV Ar+ on Si: molecular-dynamics yield 0.172 Si per ion [4]
             C4F8/Ar+ on SiO2: 1.9 A per cycle [4]; GaN/AlGaN (not Si): 1.63 A per cycle

# 5. By the numbers

  pressure          5-100 mTorr (ICP-DRIE tool spec); RIE: few to few hundred mTorr [3]
  ICP source        100-700 W in one patent example; tool spec up to 2500 W at 2.4 MHz [3]
  plasma density    ~1e11 /cm3 for a high-density plasma [3]
  cryo wafer temp   0 to -30 C, helium back-side cooling [2]
  NAND channel hole ~10 um deep through a ~100 nm opening, <10 nm deviation (<0.1% of depth) [2]
  aspect ratio      approaching 70:1 today; 100:1 described as much harder [2]
  stack             tall two-tier stack about 30 um; word-line z-pitch about 40 nm [2]
  Lam Cryo 3.0      >2x etch rate vs conventional dielectric etch, -40% energy per wafer [1]
  installed base    nearly 1,000 Lam cryo chambers in NAND fabs, 5 million wafers (vendor claims) [1]

# 8. Why deep holes are hard (HAR etch)

 ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
 │   top of hole                                  failure modes as the hole deepens [2]            │
 │   ┌──┐   ┌──┐   polymer at the mouth           * polymer at top can block ions                  │
 │   │  │   │  │   narrows the opening           * conventional RIE rate drops as holes get deeper │
 │   │  │   │  │                                 * allowed deviation <10 nm over 10 um             │
 │   │  │   │  │   ions lose energy and angle    * 30+ recipe knobs; 33 tuned by AI once [2]       │
 │   │  │   │  │   on the way down               * ~30% more word lines per generation [2]         │
 │   │  │   │  │                                                                                   │
 │   └──┘   └──┘   floor: ions + HF / F reach it, byproducts must get out                          │
 │   cold wafer (cryo): less sidewall attack, more adsorbed neutrals, faster floor etch [2]        │
 └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

# 9. Who makes it, structure, risks

  Share is only known from secondary reports: Lam + Applied about 60% of etch revenue, TEL about 20%;
  another report says the top three hold about 90%. Definitions differ; no primary split is public. [5]
  Lam Research (LRCX)       etch leader; cryo dielectric etch for NAND high-aspect-ratio holes
  Tokyo Electron (8035.T)   first cryo etcher 2023; reports 84% lower carbon vs its first cryo run [2]
  Applied Materials (AMAT)  conductor etch and selective removal; bigger in deposition
  Hitachi High-Tech         etch tools too (parent Hitachi, 6501.T); ticker is textbook, not sourced
  Risks: NAND capex swings (2023 drop above), China at 34% of Lam sales (export controls), and the
  etch-step count rising with every NAND layer and GAA node (textbook).
