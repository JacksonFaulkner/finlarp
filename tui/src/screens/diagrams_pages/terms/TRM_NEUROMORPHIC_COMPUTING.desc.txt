Neuromorphic computing builds chips that process information with spiking, event-driven circuits that place memory and compute close together, instead of moving data between separate memory and processor blocks. Intel Loihi 2 and BrainChip Akida are examples aimed at low-power inference on sparse sensor inputs. The approach is promising for edge devices, but it has a small commercial footprint and GPUs still carry most AI workloads. Related: Intel (INTC), BrainChip (BRCHF), NVIDIA (NVDA), Qualcomm (QCOM).
