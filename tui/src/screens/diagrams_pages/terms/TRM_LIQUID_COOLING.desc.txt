Liquid cooling removes heat from chips with a fluid instead of air, usually through cold plates bolted onto GPUs and CPUs (direct-to-chip) or by submerging servers in dielectric fluid (immersion). A coolant distribution unit (CDU) pumps the loop and hands heat to the facility water system. Racks of current Nvidia GPUs draw over 100 kW, which air alone cannot cool, so liquid cooling has become a requirement for new AI halls. Related: Vertiv (VRT), Schneider Electric, nVent (NVT), Modine (MOD), Super Micro (SMCI).
