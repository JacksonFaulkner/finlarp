A Mach-Zehnder modulator (MZM) splits light into two paths, shifts the phase of one or both with an applied voltage, and recombines them so the beams add or cancel. This turns an electrical data signal into light intensity or phase changes. MZMs are built in silicon photonics, indium phosphide and lithium niobate, and are the standard modulator for coherent optics and many 800G-1.6T transceivers. They are larger and use more drive power than micro-ring modulators but are more tolerant of temperature. Related: Coherent (COHR), Lumentum (LITE), Marvell (MRVL), Broadcom (AVGO).
