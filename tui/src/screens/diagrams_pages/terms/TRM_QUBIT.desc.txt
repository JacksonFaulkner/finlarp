A qubit is the basic unit of quantum information, a two-level quantum system that can hold a superposition of 0 and 1 until it is measured. Common physical forms are superconducting circuits, trapped ions and photonic modes. Useful quantum computers need many physical qubits per logical qubit because today's qubits are noisy and short-lived, which keeps the field years away from broad commercial workloads.
