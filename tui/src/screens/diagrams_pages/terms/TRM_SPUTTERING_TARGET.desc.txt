A sputtering target is a solid disc or plate of metal, alloy, or ceramic that is bombarded by plasma ions in a vacuum chamber, which knocks atoms off the target and deposits them as a thin film on a wafer. Common targets are copper, tantalum, titanium, aluminum, and tungsten, and each must meet tight purity and grain-size specs. Chip makers and display makers use physical vapor deposition (PVD) with these targets to form the metal layers that wire up transistors and memory. Target quality sets film uniformity, so it is a steady consumable in fab output. Related: Materion (MTRN), Entegris (ENTG), Applied Materials (AMAT)
