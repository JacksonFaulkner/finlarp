Backside power delivery moves the power wiring of a chip to the back of the wafer, leaving the front-side metal stack for signals only. It cuts voltage droop and frees routing space, which helps dense, power-hungry AI accelerators run at higher clocks. Intel ships it as PowerVia on 18A, TSMC plans Super Power Rail on A16, and Samsung has its own version on 2nm-class nodes. It needs wafer thinning and bonding steps that add work for equipment and bonding vendors. Related: Intel (INTC), Taiwan Semiconductor (TSM), Applied Materials (AMAT), Lam Research (LRCX).
