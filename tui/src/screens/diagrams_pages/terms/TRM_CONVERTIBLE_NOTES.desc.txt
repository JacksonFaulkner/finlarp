Convertible notes are bonds that the holder can exchange for a set number of shares, usually at a conversion price well above the current stock price when issued. They carry a low coupon because the conversion option is worth something to the buyer. If the stock rises past the conversion price, the notes add new shares and dilute existing holders, which is why companies often buy capped calls to offset it.
