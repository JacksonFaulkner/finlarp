Thin-film lithium niobate (TFLN) is a modulator material made by bonding a sub-micron layer of lithium niobate crystal onto a silicon or insulator wafer. Lithium niobate changes its refractive index linearly with voltage (the Pockels effect), so TFLN modulators are fast, low-loss and need low drive voltage. Bulk lithium niobate modulators have long been used in telecom; the thin-film form shrinks them toward chip scale for 200G-per-lane and coherent applications. It competes with silicon photonics and indium phosphide modulators. Related: Coherent (COHR), Lumentum (LITE), HyperLight (private), Fujitsu (private optical unit).
