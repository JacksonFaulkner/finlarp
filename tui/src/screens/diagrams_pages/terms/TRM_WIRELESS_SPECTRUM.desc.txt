Wireless spectrum is the range of radio frequencies that carriers are licensed to use, and it is the scarce input that limits how much capacity a network can sell. The FCC assigns licenses through auctions, and the amounts paid are recorded as intangible assets and as cash outflows. Low-band spectrum gives coverage, and mid-band and mmWave give capacity, so carriers buy different bands for different jobs. Related: AT&T (T), Verizon (VZ), T-Mobile US (TMUS), EchoStar (SATS).
