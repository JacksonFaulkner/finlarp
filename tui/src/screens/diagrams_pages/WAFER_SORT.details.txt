== PROBER
# The robot that steps the wafer
The prober loads wafers from a FOUP, finds the notch, pre-aligns optically and vacuums the wafer onto the chuck. Cameras then align the pads and the chuck indexes die to die, lifting in Z to bring the needles into contact (page). Tokyo Electron (8035.T) and Accretech / Tokyo Seimitsu (7729.T) are the listed prober makers, with no sourced share.

# Why stepping accuracy matters
HBM micro-bump probing needs pitch below 60 um (FormFactor deck), so alignment errors of a few microns matter. Probers also hold chuck temperature for hot or cold test.

With 128 to 256 HBM sites contacted at once (FormFactor deck), the chuck must stay planar and the prober needs high Z force, since many probes push at once (textbook inference). Test time per die has no sourced number on the page and varies from milliseconds to seconds by device.

== PROBE CARD
# The consumable that touches the die
The probe card is a custom board with needles or MEMS springs matched to one chip's bond pad layout, so each product needs its own card. It has a PCB, a space transformer and the probes (page diagram). Card cost and lead time are part of every new chip launch (textbook).

# Probe counts and suppliers
FormFactor figures on the page: 1K to 5K probes per die for advanced packaging devices, 15K to 60K probes on an advanced packaging SoC card, HBM at 128 to 256 sites in parallel. A 1995 high-end card had 500 probes, 60 um tips and 100 um pads on 125 um pitch (Keithley note), so probe counts rose by orders of magnitude.

Technoprobe 2025 revenue was EUR 628.4M versus 543.2M in 2024 (page). One secondary report says FormFactor HBM revenue grew fourfold in fiscal 2024, and another puts Micronics Japan probe card sales at JPY 68.5 billion in fiscal 2025, up 28.0%.

== ATE TESTER
# The tester mainframe
The mainframe holds pin electronics, power supplies and timing, and drives patterns into the die through the test head and probe card (page). Tests include DC (shorts, opens, leakage), functional, speed binning and analog trims.

# The duopoly
Advantest (6857.T) and Teradyne (TER) are the duopoly; Cohu and Keysight are smaller. A blog-grade 2023 estimate on the page gave Advantest 50%, Teradyne 30%, others 20%.

Advantest's notes say FY2025 set records in sales, operating profit and net income and that the tester market grew a second year in calendar 2025 with the company gaining share. Teradyne 2025: $3.2B revenue, +13%, test about 80%, Q4 test $883M (+45%).

== WAFER MAP
# Binning and the map
The tester returns a bin number per die and the map is stored as a data file or marked by ink (page). Bin 1 is full-speed good, higher bins are slower sellable grades or hard fails. Packaging and dicing skip the bad die, so test avoids spending assembly cost on them.

# A map is a diagnostic
Clusters of failing die at the edge point to process problems, and a ring points to CMP (page's drawing notes). Systematic patterns at the same reticle position point to a mask or litho issue (textbook).

For HBM, wafer-level test is the known-good-die gate; one market report puts wafer-level testing at 44.13% of the HBM KGD test market in 2025 (research sales page, treat as indicative). The sample map on the page is illustrative, not fab data.

== space transformer
# Re-routing pad pitch to board pitch
Chip pads are tight and the probe card PCB is coarse, so the space transformer (a multilayer ceramic or organic substrate) fans out from the probe pitch to the PCB pitch (page). It sets probe layout accuracy and, at large probe counts, limits how many probes fit.

# Why MEMS probes
HBM micro-bump probing is below 60 um pitch, with 45 um fine pitch and probe force under 1.5 gf (FormFactor deck). A 1995 card used 125 um pad pitch. At 45 um pitch each probe must be tiny, which is why MEMS spring probes replaced hand-made needles (textbook).

A FormFactor presentation notes each new HBM generation adds stacked die and heat, which affects probe-to-pad alignment, so the transformer must keep its geometry across temperature. The page calls fine-pitch cards a bottleneck with a single-digit number of vendors (FormFactor, Technoprobe, MPI, CHPT, Micronics Japan).

== TEST HEAD
# Pogo pins between tester and card
The test head carries the tester's pin electronics close to the wafer, and a pogo-pin or spring-contact interface connects it to the probe card PCB (page). A short electrical path protects signal timing for fast interfaces (textbook).

# Why the path matters
Modern AI chips and HBM run high-speed I/O, so the head, pogo interface and card must hold impedance and power delivery. Accelerators draw high currents even during wafer test (textbook inference).

Teradyne and Advantest design heads with their mainframes, so the interface is vendor-specific, which adds switching cost (textbook). Teradyne semiconductor test revenue rose 19% in 2025 and Q4 test revenue was $883M, up 45% year on year (page). Worn pogo pins add contact resistance and false fails, a routine maintenance item (textbook).

== WAFER CHUCK
# Vacuum, motion and temperature
The chuck holds the wafer by vacuum on an X-Y-Z-theta stage and may be heated or cooled (page). Hot or cold test screens temperature-sensitive fails (textbook). Planarity matters because hundreds of probes push on the wafer and the contact force sums over all of them.

# The HBM stress case
With 128 to 256 sites at once and probe force under 1.5 gf each on fine-pitch pads (FormFactor deck), the total load is large yet each pad sees little, so wafer flatness and Z control must hold.

Thin wafers for stacked memory are fragile on the chuck, so vacuum and support design matter (textbook). AI chips dissipate heat during test, so chuck cooling capacity limits how hard a device can be exercised (textbook inference). No sourced figure on stage accuracy was found.

== overdrive
# Overdrive: the contact trade-off
Overdrive is extra Z travel after the tips first touch the pads. It bends the needle so the tip scrubs across the pad, breaking the native oxide. A patent on the page uses about 3 mils (about 76 um); too little overdrive gives bad contact, too much damages the pad or slides the tip off.

# Why the scrub works
Aluminium pads grow an insulating oxide, one patent puts it at about 5 to 10 nm, and it dominates contact resistance. A tungsten needle card typically needs about 0.002 to 0.004 inch of overdrive for good contact (patent).

Cantilever tips scrub more and put shear load under the pad, while vertical probes mostly apply compression (probe-test papers). Deep scrub marks make wire bonding harder, and high overdrive wears and breaks probe tips. Overdrive distance is hard to control precisely.

== HBM parallelism
# 128 to 256 sites at once
The page gives HBM parallelism of 128 to 256 sites at once from a FormFactor deck. One probe card contacts many die per touchdown, so a wafer needs fewer steps and test cost per die falls (textbook). The same deck gives HBM micro-bump probing pitch below 60 um, with fine pitch 45 um and probe force under 1.5 gf.

# Why HBM needs known-good die
Every die in a stack must be good before stacking, because a bad die ruins the whole stack, so wafer-level known-good-die testing gates yield (textbook). One market summary says FormFactor HBM revenue grew fourfold in fiscal 2024.

Micronics Japan memory probe cards hit a quarterly record in Q1 2026, with sales up 51.0% year on year (secondary summary). HBM supply therefore partly depends on probe card capacity (textbook inference).

== Repair step
# Memory repair after test
For memory, failing rows or columns are replaced with spare ones and the die is retested (page procedure, textbook). Repair is done by fusing or laser trimming links, or programming on-die fuses (textbook). Spare capacity lets a die with a few defective cells be sold as fully good, so wafer sort raises effective yield rather than just rejecting die.

# Why repair matters for HBM
DRAM die used in HBM stacks must be fully good after repair before stacking, since a bad die ruins the stack. That is the known-good-die logic behind the 128 to 256 site parallel probing on the page.

The tester drives memory test patterns and logs failing addresses, so it needs fast built-in redundancy analysis (textbook). The Advantest summary reports memory tester sales down 36% in FY2025 while SoC tester sales rose 44% (secondary summary). No sourced figure was found for repair yield uplift.
