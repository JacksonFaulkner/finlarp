== COLD PLATE
# What it does
A cold plate is a copper block clamped onto a chip with coolant running through channels inside it, taking the heat straight off the die.
# Why it matters
It is how 1,000 W-plus GPUs and 120 kW-plus racks are cooled at all; air cannot move that much heat.
# Source
Supermicro 10-K (2026); NVIDIA GB200 user guide.

== TIM
# What it does
A thermal interface material fills the microscopic gaps between the die and the cold plate so heat does not have to cross air.
# Why it matters
Though a fraction of a millimetre thick, it is often the largest single temperature drop in the path.
# Source
Textbook.

== BASE
# What it does
The base is the solid copper floor of the plate that spreads the die's concentrated heat out under the fins.
# Why it matters
Hot spots on the die only cool well if the base spreads them before the water sees them.
# Source
Textbook.

== MICROCHANNEL
# What it does
Microchannels are rows of fins about 0.1-0.2 mm thick with slots between, giving the coolant a large surface to pick heat from.
# Why it matters
Finer channels cool better but need more pump pressure and clean, filtered coolant.
# Source
Textbook.

== QUICK DISCONNECT
# What it does
A quick disconnect is a self-sealing coupling: spring poppets shut both halves before they part, so a tray can be pulled without spilling.
# Why it matters
Every liquid-cooled tray needs a pair; supply ran short as NVL72 racks ramped. OCP's UQD spec makes them interchangeable.
# Source
OCP UQD specification.

== MANIFOLD
# What it does
The rack manifold is a pair of vertical pipes, supply and return, with one quick-disconnect tap per tray.
# Why it matters
It ties every tray to the CDU; one manifold carries the whole rack's 120-130 kW of heat.
# Source
Supermicro 10-K (2026).

== CDU
# What it does
A coolant distribution unit pumps the rack loop and passes its heat to facility water through a heat exchanger.
# Why it matters
It keeps the server loop clean and at the right temperature and pressure, separate from building water.
# Source
AAON 10-K (2026).

== WARM WATER
# What it does
Warm-water cooling returns coolant at about 45-60 C, hot enough to reject to outside air with dry coolers.
# Why it matters
It cuts or removes chiller energy and makes heat reuse possible.
# Source
NVIDIA GB200 user guide; ASHRAE liquid cooling classes.
